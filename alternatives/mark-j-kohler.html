Quick verdict

Mark J. Kohler is one of the few people in this category who is both a licensed CPA and a licensed attorney, and his ecosystem lets you learn from him, hire his law firm, and open a self-directed retirement account without leaving his group of companies. For a small business owner who wants entity, tax, and legal work under one roof, he is often the right pick, and this page says so more than once.

People look elsewhere for four reasons: they are W-2 earners rather than owners, they want a dedicated 1-on-1 guide rather than group calls, they want the tax plan coordinated with debt, income, and protection, or they would rather hire a local CPA and attorney than buy into one ecosystem. Legacy Wealth Blueprint, which we publish, is the fit for the first three. Our earlier post, Karlton Dennis vs Mark Kohler vs Legacy Wealth Blueprint, covers the same ground with a different lens.

What Mark J. Kohler is

Mark J. Kohler is a senior partner at KKOS Lawyers, listed there as a licensed attorney in Utah and Idaho and a licensed CPA in California, Utah, and Idaho. Around him sits a group of companies: KKOS Lawyers for legal and entity work, Directed IRA for self-directed retirement accounts, Main Street Business Services, the Main Street Business Podcast, several books, a Tax Advisor Certification for professionals, and Business Owner HQ, a membership for small business owners with an app, on-demand videos, biweekly live masterminds with him, an AI assistant, a forum, and a resource library.

Format. Education is membership-based and group-based: recorded content plus live group calls with Kohler. Professional services are separate engagements with his firms, quoted separately. His best-known framework is the trifecta: an operating entity on one side, a holding entity for assets on the other, and a revocable living trust above both, with your personal return in the middle.

Known for

  • Teaching as a licensed CPA and attorney, which is rare in this category
  • The trifecta entity and trust structure for owners with assets to protect
  • Books on the tax and legal playbook for small business owners
  • Self-directed IRA and Solo 401(k) education, backed by Directed IRA
  • Biweekly live masterminds inside Business Owner HQ

Best for

  • Small business owners with entity questions who want live time with a CPA and attorney
  • Owners who want the legal work done by the same group that taught them
  • Investors interested in self-directed retirement accounts
  • Serious do-it-yourselfers who will read the books and act on them

Not ideal for

  • W-2 earners with no business; the content assumes an entity
  • People who want a dedicated 1-on-1 guide rather than group calls and a forum
  • Anyone who wants debt, income, and protection coached alongside tax
  • Buyers who prefer to hire professionals with no financial tie to the educator

Why people look for Mark J. Kohler alternatives

  • They earn a salary. The trifecta and the S-corp material are aimed at owners, and a W-2 earner leaves the calls with fewer moves than they hoped.
  • They want one person who knows their file and checks in, not a biweekly group call and a forum.
  • They want a written 12-month plan that covers tax, debt, accounts, and protection in sequence, not a library of answers to individual questions.
  • They would rather hire a CPA and an attorney with no connection to the person who taught them, so the advice and the sale are separate.
  • They already read the books and now need someone to hold them to a plan.
  • They are comparing him with Karlton Dennis and want a third option that is neither a firm nor an owner-only membership.

4. A local CPA plus an attorney, hired separately

Type: Firm or professional service. Best for: Owners who want the trifecta or something like it built by professionals with no financial tie to an educator.

Strengths

  • The advice and the sale are separate; nobody teaching you profits from the entity you form
  • State-specific knowledge on both the tax and the legal side
  • You can read Kohler's books, then hire people to execute exactly the parts that apply

Tradeoffs

  • Two professionals means two engagements and the coordination is on you
  • Finding a CPA who plans and an attorney who understands small-business structures takes interviews; see the S-corp guide for the questions to ask
  • No community, no curriculum, no accountability between meetings

5. Do it yourself with Kohler's books, IRS publications, and free guides

Type: Free resource. Best for: Owners who learn well from reading, have simple structures, and are willing to act without a group behind them.

Strengths

  • His books lay out the trifecta, the S-corp decision, and the self-directed account rules in plain language
  • Our guides on the S-corp strategy, the Solo 401(k), and S-corp vs the QBI deduction walk the same decisions with worked examples
  • Costs almost nothing and tells you which paid option you actually need

Tradeoffs

  • Reading is not implementing; the entity still has to be formed, the payroll still has to run, and the trust still has to be drafted
  • No accountability, and it is easy to misjudge whether a strategy applies to you
  • Rules change; a book from a few years ago will not carry the current thresholds

How to decide

  1. Name your income type. Mostly W-2: pick something that covers W-2 strategies first. Mostly business: Kohler's owner focus or a firm goes deeper on entity work.

  2. Decide whether you want answers or a plan. A membership with live Q&A answers the questions you bring. A written 12-month plan with a guide tells you the order of operations and checks that you did it.

  3. Decide how you feel about buying services from the educator. It is convenient and it is a conflict; both are true. If you want independence, hire a local CPA and attorney and use the books as your syllabus.

  4. Check the specific strategies against your facts before any call: the S-corp strategy, the Solo 401(k), and S-corp vs the QBI deduction are the three that decide most owner questions.

  5. Ask every option who answers you, whether anyone files, what is delivered in writing, and what happens when the term ends. Get it in an email.

Questions people ask

Is Mark J. Kohler a CPA or an attorney?

Both. His KKOS Lawyers profile lists him as a licensed attorney in Utah and Idaho and a licensed CPA in California, Utah, and Idaho. He is a senior partner at that firm.

What is the trifecta structure?

Kohler's framework for owners: an operating entity such as an LLC or S-corp on one side, a holding entity for assets like rentals on the other, and a revocable living trust above both, with your personal return in the middle. It is a way of organising operations, assets, and estate planning; whether you need all three pieces depends on what you own.

What is the best Mark J. Kohler alternative for a W-2 earner?

Legacy Wealth Blueprint, because it covers W-2 strategies, tax-advantaged accounts, debt, and protection in one plan rather than assuming an entity. If you own nothing yet, start free: max the retirement accounts and the HSA, then decide whether a business or a property is next.

Does Legacy Wealth Blueprint set up entities or trusts?

No. It teaches when an entity makes sense and includes an entity setup partner and an estate attorney in its partner network, but the legal work is done by those professionals or your own. KKOS Lawyers does that work inside Kohler's ecosystem, which is a real advantage if that is your main need.

Should I just read Kohler's books and hire a local CPA?

For a simple structure, yes, that is often enough. Read the books, run the S-corp and Solo 401(k) guides against your numbers, and hire a CPA who plans rather than only prepares. Buy a program when you need accountability, a written plan, or a licensed teacher on a schedule.

How does Business Owner HQ compare with Legacy Wealth Blueprint on support?

Business Owner HQ lists biweekly live masterminds with Kohler, a forum, an app, and an AI assistant. Legacy Wealth Blueprint lists weekly live coaching calls, a 1-on-1 guide with bi-weekly check-ins, a private community, an AI advisor, and a written 12-month plan. One is group access to a licensed expert; the other is a coached plan with a dedicated point of contact. Pricing for the Blueprint is discussed on the strategy call, not published.
