Stay on BiggerPockets for deals and community. Look elsewhere only when you can name the gap: a tax plan for your return, a coach for one property, or a CPA who works with landlords.

Quick verdict

BiggerPockets is the best free starting point in real estate, and for finding, analysing, and talking through deals it is still hard to beat. If that is what you need, stay. The people who go looking for an alternative usually need something the platform was never built to give them: a tax plan for their specific return, a coach who answers their questions the same day, or a CPA who actually works with landlords.

Match the alternative to the gap. A real-estate-focused CPA firm fixes the filing and planning gap. Short-term rental coaching fixes the execution gap for one property model. Legacy Wealth Blueprint fits the person who wants tax strategy, debt, accounts, and protection handled as one written plan, with property as one piece of it rather than the whole point. We publish this page and offer that program, so read our section with that in mind.

What BiggerPockets is

BiggerPockets is an online community for real estate investors that has been around since 2004. It is best known for its forums, its podcasts, its books, and its deal calculators. The free tier is large; a paid Pro membership adds calculator limits, market data, landlord tools, and other extras, and the company also sells bootcamps and books through its own publishing arm.

Format. Self-serve. You read, listen, post questions, and run numbers. Nobody on the platform knows your return, your W-2, or your entity structure unless you post the details, and forum replies are opinions from other investors, not advice from someone accountable to you.

Known for

  • Forums where thousands of active investors answer questions on every strategy from house hacking to syndications
  • The BiggerPockets Real Estate Podcast and Real Estate Rookie, both free and published several times a week
  • Calculators for rental analysis, BRRRR, fix-and-flip, and similar deal maths
  • Books on rental investing, BRRRR, and real estate tax basics
  • Bootcamps, meetups, and directories for finding agents, lenders, and tax professionals

Best for

  • Beginners who need to understand the models before spending anything
  • Investors who want to sanity-check a deal or a market with people who have done it
  • Anyone who learns well from long-form podcasts and reading
  • Finding local professionals and investor-friendly agents through the directories

Not ideal for

  • A specific tax plan for your own return, entity, and income mix
  • Accountability: nobody follows up if you stop showing up
  • Short-term rental setup where you want a coach reviewing your listing, pricing, and lease
  • People who already know the strategy and need implementation help, not more information

Why people look for BiggerPockets alternatives

  • They have listened to hundreds of episodes and still have not bought a property. Information is not the bottleneck any more; execution is.
  • They have a high W-2 income and want to know whether the short-term rental loophole, cost segregation, or real estate professional status applies to them, and forum threads give five different answers.
  • Their current CPA is a generalist who has never filed a cost segregation study or asked about material participation hours.
  • They want one person to answer questions about their deal, not a thread that goes quiet after two replies.
  • They own one or two rentals and realise the tax plan, the debt plan, and the retirement accounts were never coordinated.
  • They are drawn to short-term rentals specifically and want a program built around that model rather than general rental content.

2. A real-estate-focused CPA firm (WCG CPAs & Advisors, The Real Estate CPA)

Type: Firm or professional service. Best for: Investors who already own property and want the return prepared and the planning done by people who work with landlords every day.

Strengths

  • They file. That is the one thing no course, community, or coaching program on this list does
  • Firms like WCG publish long, detailed public guides on S corporations and rental taxation, and Hall CPA (The Real Estate CPA) publishes heavily on REPS, the short-term rental rules, and cost segregation, so you can judge their thinking before you call
  • They see hundreds of investor returns a year, which means fewer conversations where you explain what a cost segregation study is
  • Planning and preparation sit under one roof, so the strategy and the filing do not drift apart

Tradeoffs

  • Most firms have minimums and waitlists, and planning is usually a separate engagement from preparation
  • You get advice on the tax questions you bring, not a coach who checks whether you followed through on debt, accounts, or protection
  • Firm capacity is finite; response times during filing season are slower than a coaching program
  • Verify current services, licensure, and scope directly; firms change their intake rules often

3. Tax Alchemy (Karlton Dennis)

Type: Firm or professional service. Best for: Investors and business owners who want a firm to build and implement the tax plan, including entity work and cost segregation, and are comfortable being quoted on a call.

Strengths

  • Done-for-you scope: advisory, entity structuring, implementation help, real estate tax planning, cost segregation, and 1031 exchange work are all listed on its site
  • Preparation is offered to advisory clients, so the plan and the return come from the same team
  • A separate education membership, Tax Strategy Accelerator, exists for people who want to learn first

Tradeoffs

  • It is a tax firm, not a real estate community; deal analysis and market research are on you
  • The founder's public bio describes him as a tax strategist without listing a CPA or EA licence; ask which person on the team holds the licence and in which state
  • Cost segregation and bonus depreciation move tax from later to now; ask any firm to walk through the exit and recapture, not only year one
  • Our Tax Alchemy alternatives guide covers the fit in more detail

4. Short-term rental coaching (Airbnb Ascension)

Type: Coaching program. Best for: Someone who wants cash flow from one short-term rental alongside their job and wants a coach on the setup rather than a forum.

Strengths

  • Airbnb Ascension assigns a dedicated 1-on-1 STR coach for onboarding, day-to-day questions, and deal reviews, plus weekly live calls and a private operator community
  • Property Profitability Reports on a deal before you sign, a market ranking across the top 500 US markets, and landlord scripts, sublease templates, and furnishing playbooks
  • The standard program is scoped to one property over six months, which keeps the goal concrete

Tradeoffs

  • It teaches rental arbitrage, meaning you lease the property and sublet it as a short-term rental. Because you do not own the building, there is no depreciation and no STR loophole benefit from an arbitrage unit
  • If the tax benefit is the goal, you need to own the property; Legacy Investing Show runs a separate done-for-you STR acquisition service for that case, discussed on the strategy call
  • You do the market research and the landlord calls yourself, with scripts and roleplay; it is coaching, not a hands-off service
  • We publish this page and offer this program, so weigh our description accordingly

5. Local REIA meetups and investor groups

Type: Community. Best for: Investors who want deal flow, contractors, lenders, and partners in their own market.

Strengths

  • In-person relationships lead to off-market deals and referrals in a way forum threads rarely do
  • You meet the local lenders, title people, and property managers who actually work your market
  • Usually free or cheap, and a good way to test whether you like the work before spending on a program

Tradeoffs

  • Quality varies by city; some groups are pitch-heavy
  • No curriculum and no tax planning; you still need a CPA and a plan
  • The advice you get depends on who happens to be in the room

6. Free content: podcasts, YouTube, IRS publications, and this site

Type: Free resource. Best for: Anyone still deciding on a model, a market, or whether real estate is even the right lever for their tax situation.

Strengths

  • Our tax strategy library walks through the STR loophole, cost segregation, REPS, bonus depreciation, and recapture in plain English, and the free calculators let you run the numbers before you buy anything
  • IRS publications and instructions are the primary source every program on this list is paraphrasing
  • Costs nothing but time, and time spent here saves money on every paid option later

Tradeoffs

  • No accountability and no personalisation; you can read for years and change nothing
  • General content cannot tell you whether your hours, your income, or your entity qualify
  • It is easy to mistake consuming content for making progress

Where Legacy Wealth Blueprint does not fit

It does not fit if what you want is a deal pipeline, market data, or a community of people trading off-market leads. BiggerPockets and a local REIA do that job better and cheaper. It does not fit if you want your return filed; the program produces a brief for your CPA and nobody on the team contacts your CPA directly. It does not fit if you are early in your career with a modest income and no debt problem to solve, because the strategies need enough income to move real money.

It also does not fit someone who wants an STR-only program. The Blueprint teaches real estate as one pillar; if you want a coach on one short-term rental, that is Airbnb Ascension, and if you want an owned STR acquired and set up for you, that is a separate service discussed on the strategy call.

How to decide

  1. Write down the one thing BiggerPockets is not giving you. If the answer is deal flow or feedback, you do not need an alternative; you need to post a real deal and set a deadline.

  2. Check whether the tax strategies you are excited about actually apply to you. The STR loophole needs ownership and material participation. REPS needs 750 hours and more than half your working time. Read the guides and be honest about your hours.

  3. Decide whether you want knowledge, labour, or a plan. Knowledge is free content and courses. Labour is a CPA firm. A plan with someone checking in is coaching.

  4. Ask every paid option the same four questions: who answers me, do you file, what is delivered in writing, and what happens when the term ends. Get the answers in an email.

  5. Run the numbers first. Use the cost segregation calculator and the STR break-even tool so you walk into any call knowing what a property would actually do for your return.

Questions people ask

Is BiggerPockets worth it, or should I pay for a program?

For learning the models and getting feedback on deals, BiggerPockets is worth it and mostly free. Pay for a program only when you can name the gap: a tax plan for your return, a coach for one property model, or a CPA who works with investors. If you cannot name the gap, the problem is execution, and no program fixes that.

What is the best BiggerPockets alternative for tax strategy?

For filing and return-specific planning, a real-estate-focused CPA firm. For a written plan that coordinates tax with debt, accounts, and protection, with a guide checking in, Legacy Wealth Blueprint. For a firm to implement entity work and cost segregation, Tax Alchemy is one of the names people compare. None of these replaces the other; they solve different problems.

Does Legacy Wealth Blueprint teach how to find and analyse deals?

It teaches real estate step by step inside the Investment Vehicles pillar, including how a rental interacts with your tax return, but it is not a deal-finding program. For underwriting practice and market data, BiggerPockets calculators and forums are the better tool.

Can I use the short-term rental loophole with an Airbnb arbitrage unit?

No. The STR loophole depends on depreciation, and depreciation requires ownership. An arbitrage unit is leased, so it produces business income and expenses but not depreciation. Arbitrage is a cash-flow strategy; owning an STR is where the tax strategy lives.

Should I get a real estate CPA or a coaching program first?

If you already own property, get the CPA first so the current return is right. If you own nothing yet and the question is what to do with your income, a plan comes first and the CPA implements it. Many people end up with both: the program sets the strategy, the CPA files it.

How is Legacy Wealth Blueprint different from a BiggerPockets Pro membership?

Pro adds tools, data, and access on a self-serve platform. Blueprint adds people: weekly live coaching, a 1-on-1 guide with bi-weekly check-ins, a written 12-month plan, and a partner network. One is a better toolbox; the other is a coached plan. Pricing for the Blueprint is not published and is discussed on the strategy call.
