Renters insurance cost in North Carolina

North Carolina averages about $128 a year, $42 below the US average.

North Carolina renters paid about $128 a year on average in the NAIC 2021 baseline, about $42 less than the $170 US average. The state average is a baseline. Your quote depends on your city, coverage limits, deductible, and claims history.

US average: $170 a year, on the same NAIC 2021 baseline.

What is different about North Carolina

North Carolina's $128 average sits well under the US figure. Charlotte and Raleigh replacement costs differ from Wilmington wind. Hurricane spillover on the coast is not a Research Triangle story.

Most shopping conversations in North Carolina start in Charlotte, Raleigh and Wilmington.

Leases and landlord rules in North Carolina

Charlotte and Raleigh complexes require proof. Wilmington and Outer Banks adjacent rentals should treat wind and flood as two questions.

What to actually schedule

Schedule electronics. Coastal flood is separate from wind. Student housing near Chapel Hill wants roommate clarity.

A Charlotte example

Example: a Charlotte renter with $20,000 contents may sit near $128. A Wilmington renter near the water should not use that average as a storm plan.

What drives North Carolina renters insurance costs

Weather exposure is a major driver here: hurricane and severe-storm risk increases toward the coast.

Claim frequency and litigation also matter: claim frequency is moderate.

Replacement costs and carrier competition round out the picture: replacement costs are moderate, with the metro areas above the state norm, and a growing, competitive insurance market keeps the average below the national figure.

Questions about carriers or complaints go to the North Carolina Department of Insurance (www.ncdoi.gov/).
