Renters insurance cost in Idaho

Idaho averages about $103 a year, $67 below the US average.

Idaho renters paid about $103 a year on average in the NAIC 2021 baseline, about $67 less than the $170 US average. The state average is a baseline. Your quote depends on your city, coverage limits, deductible, and claims history.

US average: $170 a year, on the same NAIC 2021 baseline.

What is different about Idaho

Idaho's $103 average is among the lowest in the country. That is real. It is also a small-market, lower-limit story, not a law of physics. Boise growth, wildfire at the foothills, and a basement full of skis can still quote above $103.

Most shopping conversations in Idaho start in Boise, Meridian and Idaho Falls.

Leases and landlord rules in Idaho

Boise apartment communities usually want liability proof. Smaller towns can be informal. The lease still controls.

What to actually schedule

Schedule outdoor gear. Wildfire living expenses matter at the urban fringe. Flood along rivers is separate.

A Boise example

Example: a Boise renter with $15,000 contents and a $500 deductible can sit near $103. A North End house share stuffed with bikes and cameras should not use $103 as a contents limit.

What drives Idaho renters insurance costs

Weather exposure is a major driver here: wildfire season and winter storms are the main concerns.

Claim frequency and litigation also matter: claim frequency is low and litigation is rare.

Replacement costs and carrier competition round out the picture: replacement costs are modest outside the Boise metro, and limited carrier competition in a smaller market keeps quotes low.

Questions about carriers or complaints go to the Idaho Department of Insurance (doi.idaho.gov/).

Questions people ask about Idaho renters insurance

What the state average does and does not tell you.

Is Idaho the cheapest state for renters insurance?

It is among the lowest in the NAIC 2021 table at about $103 a year, $67 under the US average.

Do Boise landlords require coverage?

Managed properties usually do.

Does the low average mean I can skip coverage?

No. A cheap average is not a $0 contents loss.
