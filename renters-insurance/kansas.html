Renters insurance cost in Kansas

Kansas renters paid about $148 a year on average in the NAIC 2021 baseline, about $22 less than the $170 US average. The state average is a baseline; your quote depends on your city, coverage limits, deductible, and claims history.

US average: $170 a year, on the same NAIC 2021 baseline.

$148 Kansas average a year, $22 below the US average

What is different about Kansas

Kansas sits at $148, close to the US average, because hail and tornadoes are not a rumor. Overland Park replacement costs run higher than western counties. The statewide mean is a weather number more than a city-snob number.

Most shopping conversations in Kansas start in Wichita, Overland Park and Kansas City.

Leases and landlord rules in Kansas

Johnson County apartments require proof. Wichita complexes usually do too. The lease clause is the rule.

What to actually schedule

Schedule electronics. Hail deductibles on auto are a different policy. Renters still need a deductible they can pay after a storm.

A Wichita example

Example: a Wichita renter with $20,000 contents might price near $148. An Overland Park unit with nicer furniture should not copy that limit.

What drives Kansas renters insurance costs

Weather exposure is a major driver here: the state sits in severe-weather territory, with hail and tornadoes common.

Claim frequency and litigation also matter: weather claims dominate and litigation is a smaller factor.

Replacement costs and carrier competition round out the picture: replacement costs are moderate, and many carriers compete, keeping the average close to the national figure.

Questions about carriers or complaints go to the Kansas Insurance Department (insurance.kansas.gov/).
