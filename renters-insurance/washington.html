Renters insurance cost in Washington

Washington renters paid about $134 a year on average in the NAIC 2021 baseline, about $36 less than the $170 US average. The state average is a baseline; your quote depends on your city, coverage limits, deductible, and claims history.

$134 a year

Source

What is different about Washington

Washington's $134 average sits under the US figure. Seattle replacement costs and theft patterns differ from Spokane. Winter atmospheric rivers and wildfire in the east are different files. Earthquake is a Seattle conversation that the $134 mean does not buy for you.

Most shopping conversations in Washington start in Seattle, Spokane and Tacoma.

Leases and landlord rules in Washington

Seattle buildings require certificates. Tacoma complexes usually do. Spokane managed properties often match that habit.

What to actually schedule

Schedule bikes and electronics. Earthquake is separate. Flood in some river valleys is separate. Guest liability matters in shared houses.

A Seattle example

Example: a Seattle renter with $30,000 contents and two commuter bikes can price above $134. Spokane may sit closer to the average.

What drives Washington renters insurance costs

Weather exposure is a major driver here: wildfire season, winter storms, and coastal weather shape claims.

Claim frequency and litigation also matter: claim frequency is low.

Replacement costs and carrier competition round out the picture: replacement costs in the Seattle metro run above the state norm, and many carriers compete, keeping the average below the national figure.

Questions about carriers or complaints go to the Washington State Office of the Insurance Commissioner (www.insurance.wa.gov/).
