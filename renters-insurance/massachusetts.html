Renters insurance cost in Massachusetts

Massachusetts averages about $141 a year, $29 below the US average.

Massachusetts renters paid about $141 a year on average in the NAIC 2021 baseline, about $29 less than the $170 US average. The state average is a baseline. Your quote depends on your city, coverage limits, deductible, and claims history.

US average: $170 a year, on the same NAIC 2021 baseline.

What is different about Massachusetts

Massachusetts averages $141, close to the US figure, because Boston replacement costs are high while western counties pull the mean down. Nor'easters and old triple-deckers create water claims that a $141 statewide number cannot narrate.

Most shopping conversations in Massachusetts start in Boston, Worcester and Springfield.

Leases and landlord rules in Massachusetts

Boston and Cambridge buildings require certificates, often with strict liability minimums. Worcester is a little looser and still lease-driven.

What to actually schedule

Schedule bikes and electronics. Flood in a basement triple-decker is a classic Boston miss. Winter freeze is real.

A Boston example

Example: a Boston renter with $30,000 contents can price well above $141. Somerville is not Springfield.

What drives Massachusetts renters insurance costs

Weather exposure is a major driver here: winter storms and nor'easters are the primary weather risk.

Claim frequency and litigation also matter: claim frequency is low.

Replacement costs and carrier competition round out the picture: replacement costs are among the highest in the country, especially around Boston, and strong carrier competition keeps the average close to the national figure despite high costs.

Questions about carriers or complaints go to the Massachusetts Division of Insurance (www.mass.gov/orgs/division-of-insurance).

Questions people ask about Massachusetts renters insurance

What the state average does and does not tell you.

How much is renters insurance in Massachusetts?

About $141 a year in the NAIC 2021 baseline.

Do Boston landlords require it?

Almost all managed buildings do.

Does a triple-decker basement need extra water coverage?

Ask about backup of sewers and drains. Flood is still separate.

Should Worcester use Boston contents limits?

Inventory what you own. Replacement cost is not a T pass.
