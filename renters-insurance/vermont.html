Renters insurance cost in Vermont

Vermont renters paid about $113 a year on average in the NAIC 2021 baseline, about $57 less than the $170 US average. The state average is a baseline; your quote depends on your city, coverage limits, deductible, and claims history.

US average: $170 a year, on the same NAIC 2021 baseline.

$113 Vermont average a year, $57 below the US average

What is different about Vermont

Vermont's $113 average is among the lowest. Winter in Burlington still creates freeze and water claims. The cheap average is a small-market story. Lake flood and older housing still exist.

Most shopping conversations in Vermont start in Burlington, Essex and South Burlington.

Leases and landlord rules in Vermont

Burlington managed properties often require coverage. UVM-area leases may include it.

What to actually schedule

Schedule electronics and outdoor gear. Freeze claims are ordinary. Flood near the lake is separate.

A Burlington example

Example: a Burlington renter with $16,000 contents can sit near $113. A waterfront unit should ask about flood.

What drives Vermont renters insurance costs

Weather exposure is a major driver here: winter storms and cold are the main exposures.

Claim frequency and litigation also matter: claim frequency is low.

Replacement costs and carrier competition round out the picture: replacement costs are moderate, and a small carrier market keeps the average among the lowest in the country.

Questions about carriers or complaints go to the Vermont Department of Financial Regulation (dfr.vermont.gov/industry/insurance).
