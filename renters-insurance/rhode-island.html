Renters insurance cost in Rhode Island

Rhode Island renters paid about $139 a year on average in the NAIC 2021 baseline, about $31 less than the $170 US average. The state average is a baseline; your quote depends on your city, coverage limits, deductible, and claims history.

What is different about Rhode Island

Rhode Island's $139 average sits under the US figure. Providence student density and older triple-deckers create water and theft claims. The coast still cares about wind. A statewide mean on a tiny map still hides ZIP differences.

Most shopping conversations in Rhode Island start in Providence, Warwick and Cranston.

Leases and landlord rules in Rhode Island

Providence buildings near Brown and RISD often require coverage. Warwick complexes usually do too.

What to actually schedule

Schedule electronics and instruments. Coastal flood is separate. Winter freeze in triple-deckers is ordinary.

A Providence example

Example: a Providence renter with $20,000 contents and art-school equipment should not use $139 as a contents limit.

What drives Rhode Island renters insurance costs

Weather exposure is a major driver here: nor'easters and winter storms are the primary weather risk.

Claim frequency and litigation also matter: claim frequency is low.

Replacement costs and carrier competition round out the picture: replacement costs are moderate to high, and a small carrier market keeps the average right at the national figure.

Questions about carriers or complaints go to the Rhode Island Department of Business Regulation (dbr.ri.gov/insurance).

Questions people ask about Rhode Island renters insurance

How much is renters insurance in Rhode Island?
About $139 a year in the NAIC 2021 baseline.
Do Providence student leases require it?
Many do.
Does a Rhode Island policy cover nor'easters?
Wind to your stuff may be included. Flood is usually not.
Should I schedule instruments?
If they exceed the special-limits subcap, yes.
