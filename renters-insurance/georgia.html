Renters insurance cost in Georgia

Georgia renters paid about $158 a year on average in the NAIC 2021 baseline, about $12 less than the $170 US average. The state average is a baseline; your quote depends on your city, coverage limits, deductible, and claims history.

US average: $170 a year, on the same NAIC 2021 baseline.

$158 Georgia average a year, $12 below the US average

What is different about Georgia

Georgia's $158 average sits a little under the US number. Atlanta theft and replacement costs pull one way. South Georgia storm exposure pulls another. Savannah's coastal wind is not a Midtown high-rise story.

Most shopping conversations in Georgia start in Atlanta, Savannah and Augusta.

Leases and landlord rules in Georgia

Atlanta complexes require proof at move-in. Savannah historic rentals can be picky about liability because old buildings plus tourists equal slip-and-fall stories.

What to actually schedule

Schedule electronics and cameras. Hurricane spillover is more of a Savannah and Brunswick issue. Flood remains separate.

A Atlanta example

Example: an Atlanta renter in Midtown with $25,000 contents may price near $158. A beltline unit with bikes on the balcony should schedule the bikes.

What drives Georgia renters insurance costs

Weather exposure is a major driver here: severe storms and hurricane spillover from the coast shape claim patterns.

Claim frequency and litigation also matter: claim frequency is moderate, with litigation a modest factor.

Replacement costs and carrier competition round out the picture: replacement costs are moderate, with Atlanta above the state norm, and many carriers compete for Atlanta-area renters.

Questions about carriers or complaints go to the Georgia Department of Insurance (oci.georgia.gov/).
