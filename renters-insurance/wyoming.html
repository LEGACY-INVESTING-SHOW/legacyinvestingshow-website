Renters insurance cost in Wyoming

Wyoming renters paid about $106 a year on average in the NAIC 2021 baseline, about $64 less than the $170 US average. The state average is a baseline; your quote depends on your city, coverage limits, deductible, and claims history.

US average: $170 a year, on the same NAIC 2021 baseline.

$106 Wyoming average a year, $64 below the US average

What is different about Wyoming

Wyoming's $106 average is among the lowest. Jackson replacement costs and a tourism-adjacent rental market do not look like Cheyenne. Winter and wind still create claims. The cheap statewide mean is a small-market blend.

Most shopping conversations in Wyoming start in Cheyenne, Casper and Jackson.

Leases and landlord rules in Wyoming

Jackson and Cheyenne managed properties often require coverage. Casper varies more.

What to actually schedule

Schedule outdoor gear. Winter freeze is real. Wildfire living expenses matter if you are against timber. Earthquake is a quieter question than winter.

A Jackson example

Example: a Jackson renter with $30,000 of gear can price far above $106. A Cheyenne renter with modest furniture might sit near the average.

What drives Wyoming renters insurance costs

Weather exposure is a major driver here: winter storms and wildfire risk are the main exposures.

Claim frequency and litigation also matter: claim frequency is low.

Replacement costs and carrier competition round out the picture: replacement costs are moderate, and a thin carrier market keeps the average among the lowest in the country.

Questions about carriers or complaints go to the Wyoming Insurance Department (doi.wyo.gov/).
