Renters insurance cost in North Dakota

North Dakota renters paid about $117 a year on average in the NAIC 2021 baseline, about $53 less than the $170 US average. The state average is a baseline; your quote depends on your city, coverage limits, deductible, and claims history.

What is different about North Dakota

North Dakota's $117 average is low. Winter and a thin market explain more than a lack of claims. Fargo student density and river flood conversations still exist beside that cheap mean.

Most shopping conversations in North Dakota start in Fargo, Bismarck and Grand Forks.

Leases and landlord rules in North Dakota

Fargo complexes and UND-area housing often require coverage. Bismarck managed properties usually do too.

What to actually schedule

Schedule electronics. Freeze claims are the winter story. River flood is separate.

A Fargo example

Example: a Fargo renter with $15,000 contents can sit near $117. A river-adjacent unit should ask about flood, not celebrate the average.

What drives North Dakota renters insurance costs

Weather exposure is a major driver here: severe winter weather and occasional hail shape claims.

Claim frequency and litigation also matter: claim frequency is low.

Replacement costs and carrier competition round out the picture: replacement costs are moderate, and a thin carrier market keeps the average below the national baseline.

Questions about carriers or complaints go to the North Dakota Insurance Department (www.insurance.nd.gov/).

Questions people ask about North Dakota renters insurance

How much is renters insurance in North Dakota?
About $117 a year in the NAIC 2021 baseline.
Do Fargo landlords require it?
Many managed properties do.
Does the policy cover frozen pipes?
Sudden water is often covered. Vacant-and-unheated is the dispute.
Should Grand Forks renters buy flood?
If you are in a flood-exposed area, ask. It is not in the $117 average.
