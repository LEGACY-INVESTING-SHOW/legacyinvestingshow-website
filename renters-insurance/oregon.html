Renters insurance cost in Oregon

Oregon renters paid about $119 a year on average in the NAIC 2021 baseline, about $51 less than the $170 US average. The state average is a baseline; your quote depends on your city, coverage limits, deductible, and claims history.

$119 a year
US average
$170 a year
Source
NAIC 2021 baseline

What is different about Oregon

Oregon's $119 average sits well under the US figure. Portland replacement costs and theft patterns differ from Eugene student housing. Wildfire at the Gorge and in the east is a living-expense story that a $119 mean does not advertise.

Most shopping conversations in Oregon start in Portland, Eugene and Salem.

Leases and landlord rules in Oregon

Portland buildings usually require certificates. Eugene campus leases often include a coverage clause.

What to actually schedule

Schedule bikes. Wildfire smoke and evacuation need living-expense limits you could actually use. Flood in some river towns is separate.

A Portland example

Example: a Portland renter with $22,000 contents and two nice bikes may price above $119. Eugene could sit closer to the average with thinner limits.

What drives Oregon renters insurance costs

Weather exposure is a major driver here: wildfire risk, winter storms, and coastal weather all play a role.

Claim frequency and litigation also matter: claim frequency is low.

Replacement costs and carrier competition round out the picture: replacement costs in the Portland metro are above the state norm, and a competitive west-coast carrier market keeps the average below the national figure.

Questions about carriers or complaints go to the Oregon Division of Financial Regulation (dfr.oregon.gov/insurance/).
