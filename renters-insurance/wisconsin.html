Renters insurance cost in Wisconsin

Wisconsin renters paid about $112 a year on average in the NAIC 2021 baseline, about $58 less than the $170 US average. The state average is a baseline; your quote depends on your city, coverage limits, deductible, and claims history.

US average: $170 a year, on the same NAIC 2021 baseline.

$112 Wisconsin average a year, $58 below the US average

What is different about Wisconsin

Wisconsin's $112 average is well under the US figure thanks to a competitive regional market. Madison student density and Milwaukee older housing still create water and theft claims. Hail happens.

Most shopping conversations in Wisconsin start in Milwaukee, Madison and Green Bay.

Leases and landlord rules in Wisconsin

Madison campus leases often require coverage. Milwaukee complexes usually want proof. Green Bay managed properties often do too.

What to actually schedule

Schedule electronics and bikes. Freeze and ice-dam water are winter stories. Lake flood is a separate Milwaukee-edge question.

A Madison example

Example: a Madison renter with four roommates and four bikes should not use $112 as a contents limit. A Green Bay one-bedroom might sit near the average.

What drives Wisconsin renters insurance costs

Weather exposure is a major driver here: winter storms, hail, and severe weather drive claims.

Claim frequency and litigation also matter: claim frequency is moderate.

Replacement costs and carrier competition round out the picture: replacement costs are modest to moderate, and a strong regional carrier market keeps the average well below the national figure.

Questions about carriers or complaints go to the Wisconsin Office of the Commissioner of Insurance (oci.wi.gov/).
