Renters insurance cost in New Jersey

New Jersey averages about $125 a year, $45 below the US average.

New Jersey renters paid about $125 a year on average in the NAIC 2021 baseline, about $45 less than the $170 US average. The state average is a baseline. Your quote depends on your city, coverage limits, deductible, and claims history.

US average: $170 a year, on the same NAIC 2021 baseline.

What is different about New Jersey

New Jersey's $125 average is well under the US figure despite NYC-orbit replacement costs in Hoboken and Jersey City. The statewide mean includes cheaper southern counties. A Hudson County high-rise is not a Vineland rental.

Most shopping conversations in New Jersey start in Newark, Jersey City and Hoboken.

Leases and landlord rules in New Jersey

Hoboken and Jersey City buildings require certificates with specific liability. Shore seasonal landlords may be sloppier on paper and nastier after a storm.

What to actually schedule

Schedule bikes and electronics. Superstorm-style flood is not a standard renters perk. Theft in transit buildings is a police-report habit.

A Hoboken example

Example: a Hoboken renter with $35,000 contents can price well above $125. The average is a state blend.

What drives New Jersey renters insurance costs

Weather exposure is a major driver here: coastal storms and nor'easters are the headline weather risk.

Claim frequency and litigation also matter: claim frequency is moderate, with litigation more common in the metro counties.

Replacement costs and carrier competition round out the picture: replacement costs in the New York metro are high, and a deep competitive carrier market keeps the average below the national figure.

Questions about carriers or complaints go to the New Jersey Department of Banking and Insurance (www.nj.gov/dobi/division_insurance/).
