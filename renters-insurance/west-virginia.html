Renters insurance cost in West Virginia

West Virginia renters paid about $133 a year on average in the NAIC 2021 baseline, about $37 less than the $170 US average. The state average is a baseline; your quote depends on your city, coverage limits, deductible, and claims history.

US average: $170 a year, on the same NAIC 2021 baseline.

$133 West Virginia average a year, $37 below the US average

What is different about West Virginia

West Virginia's $133 average sits under the US figure. Flood in hollows and along rivers is the coverage people confuse with renters. Morgantown student housing is a different theft and roommate file than Charleston.

Most shopping conversations in West Virginia start in Charleston, Huntington and Morgantown.

Leases and landlord rules in West Virginia

Morgantown campus-area leases often require coverage. Charleston complexes usually do too.

What to actually schedule

Schedule electronics. Flood is the extra conversation. Hillside water and mine-subsidence adjacent issues are local questions for an agent, not for a national average.

A Morgantown example

Example: a Morgantown renter with $15,000 contents may sit near $133. A riverside unit in Charleston should ask about flood.

What drives West Virginia renters insurance costs

Weather exposure is a major driver here: flooding and severe storms are the main weather exposures.

Claim frequency and litigation also matter: claim frequency is moderate.

Replacement costs and carrier competition round out the picture: replacement costs are modest, and a limited carrier market keeps the average below the national figure.

Questions about carriers or complaints go to the West Virginia Offices of the Insurance Commissioner (www.wvinsurance.gov/).
