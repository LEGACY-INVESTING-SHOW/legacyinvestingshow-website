Renters insurance cost in Michigan

Michigan renters paid about $122 a year on average in the NAIC 2021 baseline, about $48 less than the $170 US average. The state average is a baseline; your quote depends on your city, coverage limits, deductible, and claims history.

Source

What is different about Michigan

Michigan's $122 average is well under the US number. Winter, water, and theft patterns differ between Detroit, Grand Rapids, and Ann Arbor student housing. Auto insurance fame in Michigan does not set renters prices, but the same winter still bursts pipes.

Most shopping conversations in Michigan start in Detroit, Grand Rapids and Ann Arbor.

Leases and landlord rules in Michigan

Ann Arbor leases near campus usually require coverage. Grand Rapids complexes do too. Detroit professionally managed buildings want certificates.

What to actually schedule

Schedule electronics. Freeze and theft are the everyday pair. Flood is still flood.

A Ann Arbor example

Example: an Ann Arbor renter with four laptops should not use $122 as a contents limit. A Grand Rapids one-bedroom with modest furniture might sit near the average.

What drives Michigan renters insurance costs

Weather exposure is a major driver here: winter weather and severe storms are the main claim triggers.

Claim frequency and litigation also matter: claim frequency is moderate.

Replacement costs and carrier competition round out the picture: replacement costs are moderate, and a competitive carrier market keeps rates below the national average.

Questions about carriers or complaints go to the Michigan Department of Insurance and Financial Services (www.michigan.gov/difs).
