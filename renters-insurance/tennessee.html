Renters insurance cost in Tennessee

Tennessee renters paid about $138 a year on average in the NAIC 2021 baseline, about $32 less than the $170 US average. The state average is a baseline; your quote depends on your city, coverage limits, deductible, and claims history.

US average: $170 a year, on the same NAIC 2021 baseline.

$138 Tennessee average a year, $32 below the US average

What is different about Tennessee

Tennessee's $138 average sits under the US figure. Nashville replacement costs and tourism-adjacent theft patterns differ from Memphis. Knoxville student housing is a third file. Tornado risk is statewide enough that a cute average should not make you skip the deductible conversation.

Most shopping conversations in Tennessee start in Nashville, Memphis and Knoxville.

Leases and landlord rules in Tennessee

Nashville complexes require proof. Memphis professionally managed properties usually do. Knoxville campus leases often include a clause.

What to actually schedule

Schedule electronics and instruments. Tornado contents claims are real. Flood in low Memphis and Nashville pockets is separate.

A Nashville example

Example: a Nashville renter in East Nashville with $25,000 contents and guitars should schedule the instruments. The $138 average is not a studio inventory.

What drives Tennessee renters insurance costs

Weather exposure is a major driver here: severe storms and tornadoes are the main claim triggers.

Claim frequency and litigation also matter: claim frequency is moderate.

Replacement costs and carrier competition round out the picture: replacement costs are moderate, and higher in Nashville, and steady carrier competition keeps the average below the national figure.

Questions about carriers or complaints go to the Tennessee Department of Commerce and Insurance (www.tn.gov/commerce/insurance.html).
