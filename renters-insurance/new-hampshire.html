Renters insurance cost in New Hampshire

New Hampshire renters paid about $104 a year on average in the NAIC 2021 baseline, about $66 less than the $170 US average. The state average is a baseline; your quote depends on your city, coverage limits, deductible, and claims history.

$104 a year

Source
NAIC 2021 baseline

What is different about New Hampshire

New Hampshire's $104 average is among the lowest. Winter still bursts pipes in Manchester mill buildings. The cheap average is a small-market story, not a law against ice.

Most shopping conversations in New Hampshire start in Manchester, Nashua and Concord.

Leases and landlord rules in New Hampshire

Manchester and Nashua managed properties usually want proof. Concord varies.

What to actually schedule

Schedule electronics. Freeze and water are the claims. Coastal flood in seacoast towns is separate.

A Manchester example

Example: a Manchester renter with $16,000 contents can sit near $104. A Portsmouth unit near the water should not treat that as flood cover.

What drives New Hampshire renters insurance costs

Weather exposure is a major driver here: winter storms are the main weather exposure.

Claim frequency and litigation also matter: claim frequency is low and litigation is rare.

Replacement costs and carrier competition round out the picture: replacement costs are moderate, and a small, stable carrier market keeps the average among the lowest in the country.

Questions about carriers or complaints go to the New Hampshire Insurance Department (www.nh.gov/insurance/).

Questions people ask about New Hampshire renters insurance

Is New Hampshire cheap for renters insurance?
The $104 NAIC 2021 average is $66 under the US figure, among the lowest.
Do Manchester landlords require coverage?
Managed properties usually do.
Does a New Hampshire policy cover frozen pipes?
Sudden water is often covered. Unheated vacancy is the fight.
Should seacoast renters buy flood?
Ask. The $104 average does not include it.
