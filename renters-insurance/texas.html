Renters insurance cost in Texas

Texas averages about $250 a year, $80 above the US average.

Texas renters paid about $250 a year on average in the NAIC 2021 baseline, about $80 more than the $170 US average. The state average is a baseline. Your quote depends on your city, coverage limits, deductible, and claims history.

US average: $170 a year, on the same NAIC 2021 baseline.

What is different about Texas

Texas has the highest statewide average in the NAIC 2021 table at about $250 a year, $80 above the US figure. Hail, wind, freeze events, hurricane exposure on the coast, and a busy claims environment all show up. Austin is not Amarillo. Houston flood conversations are not Dallas hail conversations. The $250 mean is a warning light, not a quote.

Most shopping conversations in Texas start in Houston, Dallas and Austin.

Leases and landlord rules in Texas

Austin, Dallas, and Houston apartment communities almost always require proof of insurance and additional-insured wording before keys. Student and medical-center housing is not softer. A private landlord in a smaller city may be less formal. The lease still wins.

What to actually schedule

Schedule electronics. The 2021 freeze taught a lot of renters that water from burst pipes is a contents claim with a deductible. Hurricane wind and flood are different products on the coast. Hail does not care that your sofa was cheap.

A Houston example

Example, not a statistic: a Houston renter with $25,000 contents, $100,000 liability, and a $1,000 deductible can still see a quote near or above the $250 state average after a rough storm year. An Austin renter in a new complex might beat that number. A coastal unit that skips flood coverage is not 'saving' $250. It is leaving the expensive peril unbought.

What drives Texas renters insurance costs

Weather exposure is a major driver here: the state's size means hail, tornadoes, hurricanes, and winter freezes all drive claims.

Claim frequency and litigation also matter: claim frequency and litigation are both elevated, which pushes costs up across the board.

Replacement costs and carrier competition round out the picture: replacement costs vary widely but run high in the major metros, and a huge carrier market competes hard, yet the average is the highest in the country.

Questions about carriers or complaints go to the Texas Department of Insurance (www.tdi.texas.gov/).

Questions people ask about Texas renters insurance

What the state average does and does not tell you.

Why is Texas the most expensive state for renters insurance?

The $250 NAIC 2021 average is the highest in the table because of hail, wind, freeze claims, coastal exposure, and market conditions, not because every apartment is luxury.

Do Austin and Dallas landlords require renters insurance?

Almost all professionally managed communities do, and they want a certificate naming them.

Does Texas renters insurance cover hurricane flooding in Houston?

Wind damage to your stuff may be included. Flood from storm surge or overflowing bayous is usually a separate policy.

Should I treat $250 as my quote?

No. Use it as a statewide band. Then inventory contents, pick a deductible you can pay, and quote your ZIP. Idaho's $103 average is the other end of the same table, which is how wide this market runs.
