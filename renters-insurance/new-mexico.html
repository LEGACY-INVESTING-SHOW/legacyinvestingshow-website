Renters insurance cost in New Mexico

New Mexico averages about $130 a year, $40 below the US average.

New Mexico renters paid about $130 a year on average in the NAIC 2021 baseline, about $40 less than the $170 US average. The state average is a baseline. Your quote depends on your city, coverage limits, deductible, and claims history.

US average: $170 a year, on the same NAIC 2021 baseline.

What is different about New Mexico

New Mexico's $130 average sits under the US number. Albuquerque theft and wildfire at the mountain edge matter more than desert heat. Santa Fe replacement costs for artisan furniture can exceed the statewide feel.

Most shopping conversations in New Mexico start in Albuquerque, Santa Fe and Las Cruces.

Leases and landlord rules in New Mexico

Albuquerque complexes usually require proof. Santa Fe long-term rentals often do too.

What to actually schedule

Schedule jewelry and art. Wildfire living expenses matter in the foothills. Flood from arroyos is still flood.

A Albuquerque example

Example: an Albuquerque renter with $18,000 contents may sit near $130. A Santa Fe renter with art should schedule it.

What drives New Mexico renters insurance costs

Weather exposure is a major driver here: wildfire and severe-weather events shape the claim profile.

Claim frequency and litigation also matter: claim frequency is low to moderate.

Replacement costs and carrier competition round out the picture: replacement costs are moderate, and a modest carrier market with moderate competition keeps rates near the national average.

Questions about carriers or complaints go to the New Mexico Office of Superintendent of Insurance (www.osi.state.nm.us/).
