Renters insurance cost in Minnesota

Minnesota renters paid about $139 a year on average in the NAIC 2021 baseline, about $31 less than the $170 US average. The state average is a baseline; your quote depends on your city, coverage limits, deductible, and claims history.

What is different about Minnesota

Minnesota's $139 average sits under the US figure. Hail, winter, and Twin Cities replacement costs do the work. Rochester medical-renter inventories look different from a Duluth cabin sublet.

Most shopping conversations in Minnesota start in Minneapolis, Saint Paul and Rochester.

Leases and landlord rules in Minnesota

Minneapolis and Saint Paul buildings require proof. Roommate endorsements are normal in Uptown and near campus.

What to actually schedule

Schedule electronics and bikes. Ice-dam water is a contents story. Hail is a deductible story.

A Minneapolis example

Example: a Minneapolis renter with $22,000 contents may price near $139. After a hailstorm, you will care about the deductible, not the NAIC table.

What drives Minnesota renters insurance costs

Weather exposure is a major driver here: hail, winter storms, and severe weather drive claims.

Claim frequency and litigation also matter: claim frequency is moderate and litigation is less of a factor.

Replacement costs and carrier competition round out the picture: replacement costs in the Twin Cities run above the state norm, and many carriers compete in the upper Midwest market.

Questions about carriers or complaints go to the Minnesota Department of Commerce (mn.gov/commerce/insurance/).
