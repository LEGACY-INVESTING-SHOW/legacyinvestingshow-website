Money Guy FOO vs Dave Ramsey Baby Steps

Same homework: what to do with the next dollar. Different pause on investing.

The Money Guy Financial Order of Operations, or FOO, is a nine-step order for your next dollar. Dave Ramsey's Baby Steps are a seven-step order that pauses investing until consumer debt is gone. FOO takes the employer 401(k) match before high-interest debt, then builds cash, then Roth and HSA, then work plans, and pays the mortgage last. Ramsey wants a $1,000 starter fund, then a debt snowball on everything but the house, then three to six months of cash, then 15 percent to retirement. Neither is a tax plan. Pick the order that matches the fire in your house.

What each product actually is

The FOO guide we opened was marked last updated July 29, 2026. The nine steps on that page are: deductibles, employer match, high-interest debt, emergency fund, Roth IRA and HSA, max employer plans, hyperaccumulation, prepay future expenses, then prepay low-interest debt. Hyperaccumulation here means saving a large share of pay after the tax-favored accounts are full. An HSA is a health savings account you can use with a high-deductible health plan.

The Baby Steps page lists: $1,000 starter fund; pay off all debt except the house with the debt snowball; three to six months of expenses; invest 15 percent for retirement; kids' college; pay the home off early; then build wealth and give. A debt snowball means you list debts from smallest balance to largest and attack the small one first while making minimums on the rest.

Financial Peace University is the nine-week class that teaches that order. The class page said $99.99 per household, spouse included, on September 15, 2026. The Baby Steps themselves are free to read.

If you already earn a lot

A high W-2 wage does not cancel 22 percent credit cards. If those are still on, Ramsey's pause on extra investing is often the cheaper lesson. FOO still wants the employer match first because a match is part of pay.

If cards are gone and the leftover fight is a cheap mortgage versus a 401(k), that is not this page. Use pay off the mortgage vs invest and 401(k) vs a rental. FOO vs Ramsey is the habit order. Those pages are the dollar math.

Neither order is a tax strategy class. After the accounts are full, the leak is often tax. See tax strategies for W-2 employees.

How to read seller claims

Ramsey Solutions advertises average class results, such as debt paid in 90 days. Those are their marketing lines. We did not audit them. Money Guy's learn page called $49 a sale from $249. Confirm the live checkout. We do not promise either plan will make you rich.

Questions people ask

Should a high earner follow Ramsey or FOO?

Follow Ramsey's pause if lifestyle debt is still the fire. Follow FOO's match-first step if you would otherwise skip free 401(k) money. After cards are gone, neither order replaces a tax pro.

Does FOO say to invest while in credit-card debt?

FOO puts the employer match before high-interest debt, then attacks that debt in step three. It is not 'invest in everything while cards compound.' Read the live nine steps.

Is this the same as pay off the mortgage vs invest?

No. That scorecard is mortgage math. This page is which published order you follow for the next paycheck.
