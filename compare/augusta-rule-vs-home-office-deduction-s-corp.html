Augusta Rule vs Home Office Deduction for S-Corp Owners

A scenario-driven comparison for S-corp owners deciding between Augusta Rule rent treatment and accountable-plan home office reimbursement.

Quick Verdict: Context-dependent. Most S-corp owners do not need an either/or answer. The real decision is whether your facts support one method cleanly, both methods in different contexts, or neither because documentation and usage patterns are weak. This page focuses on audit-defensible execution, not headline write-off numbers.

When Augusta Rule Usually Wins

Best for owners with legitimate periodic business meetings hosted at home and strong documentation discipline.

When Home Office via Accountable Plan Usually Wins

Best for owners with consistent exclusive business-use space and recurring reimbursable home-office expenses.

Most Common Mistake

Optimizing for the biggest deduction headline instead of the most reliable after-tax outcome for your actual facts.

Worked Example (Scenario Model)

Profile: S-corp consultant, $220k owner comp, 1,500 sq ft home, 180 sq ft office.

  • 12 valid internal strategy meetings hosted at home
  • $750 fair-market daily rent from local comps
  • $14,400 annual eligible home office costs under accountable plan

Augusta Rule Outcome

Augusta Rule yields $9,000 annual rent treatment if documentation is clean.

Home Office via Accountable Plan Outcome

Accountable plan yields roughly $1,728 annual reimbursement (12% of eligible costs).

Scenario takeaway: Augusta can produce higher nominal value in this case, but only if valuation and meeting substantiation are robust. Accountable plan is usually lower-dollar but easier to sustain annually.

Edge Cases That Change the Decision

  • Owner claims Augusta Rule days but has no calendar invites, agenda, or minutes.
  • Rent rate is copied from luxury short-term listings rather than comparable meeting rent.
  • Home office includes guest bedroom use, weakening exclusive-use support.
  • S-corp reimburses without a written accountable plan or timely substantiation.

When Not to Use This Strategy

Avoid Augusta Rule If...

  • You cannot support fair-market rental comps.
  • You do not host real business meetings at home.
  • You are using Augusta Rule as a pure year-end tax plug.

Avoid Home Office via Accountable Plan If...

  • Your office space is not exclusively used for business.
  • You cannot maintain recurring reimbursement documentation.
  • You are unwilling to run accountable-plan processes consistently.

30-Day Implementation Checklist

  • Draft or update written accountable plan policy.
  • Capture square-footage and room-use evidence with date stamps.
  • Create meeting-log template with attendees, purpose, and outcomes.
  • Save local rental comps supporting any Augusta Rule day rate.
  • Run quarterly documentation audit before reimbursement/payment.

Questions to Ask Your CPA/Advisor

  • How should we benchmark fair-market rent for my specific home and meeting profile?
  • What documentation standard do you want before booking Augusta Rule payments?
  • Can we operate both methods in non-overlapping fact patterns without double counting?
  • What are the top audit triggers for this setup in my income bracket?

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use both approaches in the same year?

Sometimes yes, if facts are distinct and expenses are not duplicated. Your CPA should map boundaries clearly before year-end.

Is Augusta Rule always bigger?

No. It can be larger in some cases but fails quickly when valuation and meeting records are weak.

What breaks home office deductions most often?

Mixed-use space and weak accountable-plan execution are common failure points.
