Cost Segregation vs Bonus Depreciation

When a cost segregation study is worth the fee versus taking bonus depreciation only on assets you already have on invoices, with recapture and usable-loss gates.

Who this page is for

Investors who just bought or renovated a building and need to know whether to pay for an engineering study.

These two labels get sold as rivals. They are not. Cost segregation reclassifies building cost into shorter lives. Bonus depreciation is the first-year percentage that then applies to property with a recovery period of 20 years or less. The real decision is whether the study fee buys enough extra short-life basis, and whether you can use the deduction this year.

Read this before you decide The study creates buckets. Bonus is the first-year rate on those buckets.

When Cost Segregation Wins

The study wins when the building is large enough, the purchase or renovation is recent, the extra short-life basis is real, and you have a documented way to use the loss this year or on a short, planned timeline.

When Bonus Depreciation Wins

Bonus without a study wins when the obvious personal property and improvement invoices already capture most of the acceleration, the building is too small for the fee, or you expect to sell before the study can earn its cost.

Where People Lose Money

Paying $8,000 to $15,000 for a study, celebrating a six-figure year-one number, then discovering most of it carries forward because the rental is passive and you have no participation path.

Executive Summary

A lot of owners hear cost segregation and bonus depreciation in the same sales pitch and think they have to pick a side. The study is how you find 5-year carpet, 7-year equipment, and 15-year site work inside a 27.5-year or 39-year building. Bonus is the timing rule that can pull a large share of those shorter-life costs into year one.

If you already have clean invoices for furniture, appliances, and qualified improvement property, you may take bonus on those items without a study. The study earns its keep when a meaningful slice of the building itself can be reclassified, and when the tax on that extra depreciation exceeds the fee after you apply passive-loss rules.

Paper depreciation is not cash. If the loss is trapped as passive, you paid for a carryforward. Model usable loss before you sign the engagement letter.

Exit math matters as much as year-one math. Accelerated depreciation can come back as ordinary income on a sale. A two-year flip and a ten-year hold are different decisions even when the study looks identical.

Decision Framework (Execution-First)

Price the study against extra usable depreciation, then check hold period and recapture. Do not start with the marketing range.

  1. Write the building basis after land, the close date or placed-in-service date, and the expected hold period.
  2. List assets that already qualify for bonus without a study, using invoices and a depreciation schedule.
  3. Ask your CPA how much of a year-one loss you can actually use under passive-activity rules, STR participation, or real estate professional status.
  4. Get a written study quote and a conservative reclassification range, then run tax at your marginal rate on the extra usable deduction minus the fee.
  5. Model a sale in year 3 and year 8, including recapture, before you authorize the study.

Worked Example (Scenario Model)

Profile: Raleigh investors close a $1,150,000 fourplex. Land is 20 percent. Building basis is $920,000. They expect an 8-year hold and have STR participation logs.

  • Study quote is $8,500 and reclassifies $210,000 to 5-year property and $90,000 to 15-year property, leaving $620,000 on 27.5 years
  • Model bonus rate is 60 percent of qualifying short-life basis, matching the current site figure and subject to the filing-year statute
  • Federal-plus-state marginal rate on usable ordinary income is 35 percent
  • Without a study, year-one building depreciation is about $33,500 ($920,000 divided by 27.5)

Cost Segregation outcome

With the study and 60 percent bonus, year-one depreciation is about $180,000 of bonus on the $300,000 short-life pool, plus about $22,500 on the remaining $620,000, or roughly $202,500 before the $8,500 fee. If the $169,000 of extra deduction is usable, tax savings are about $59,000.

Bonus Depreciation outcome

Bonus alone, with no study, stays near the $33,500 straight-line figure unless they have a separate furniture invoice. If they later add $40,000 of appliances, bonus on that invoice is $24,000. That is real money and it does not require an engineering report.

Scenario takeaway: The study is a purchase of extra short-life basis. Buy it when usable tax on that extra basis clears the fee with room for recapture. Skip it when invoices already grab the acceleration or the loss will sit unused.

Edge Cases That Change the Decision

  • You run look-back cost segregation on a property placed in service years ago and need Form 3115 treatment, not a casual year-one claim.
  • Land value is understated so the study inflates depreciable basis.
  • You sell inside three years and ordinary-income recapture plus the fee wipe the headline savings.
  • A weak report uses percentages with no site visit and no asset list you can defend.

When Not to Use This Strategy

Avoid Cost Segregation if...

  • Building basis after land is well under $500,000 and the fee will eat the tax savings.
  • You have no path to use a passive loss this year or next.
  • You already plan to list the property in the next 24 months.

Avoid Bonus Depreciation if...

  • You treat bonus as a substitute for identifying building components that are still sitting on 27.5 or 39 years.
  • You claim bonus on structural building cost that does not qualify.
  • You model 100 percent bonus in a year when the statute uses a lower rate.

  • Run post-implementation review, compare projected vs actual results, and adjust the playbook for next quarter.

Questions to Ask Your CPA/Advisor

  • How much extra short-life basis do you need before a study fee is rational on this building?
  • What bonus percentage should we model for the year the property is placed in service?
  • If the rental stays passive, what is the carryforward worth on my timeline?
  • How should we model recapture if I sell in year 3 versus year 8?

What to include in your advisor packet

  • A one-page objective memo clarifying what "winning" means for this decision (Cost Segregation vs Bonus Depreciation).
  • Baseline and alternative math model with all assumptions clearly listed.
  • Supporting evidence folder for qualification, valuations, logs, and policy records.
  • Risk memo covering edge cases, red flags, and fallback plan if assumptions fail.
  • Annual review checklist showing what will be re-evaluated before next filing cycle.
