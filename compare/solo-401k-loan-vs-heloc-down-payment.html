Solo 401(k) Loan vs HELOC for Down Payment (Tax + Liquidity Edge Cases)

A practical financing comparison for self-employed buyers funding down payments under liquidity pressure.

Executive Summary

Bottom line: This is a capital-stack decision, not a rate-shopping exercise. You need to model repayment risk, business volatility, and opportunity cost together before selecting a funding source.

Solo 401(k) Loan tends to win when Best when retirement plan rules permit loan terms and business cash flow supports repayment certainty.

HELOC tends to win when Best when collateral capacity exists and loan flexibility is more valuable than retirement-plan borrowing constraints.

This guide is intentionally built for edge-case implementation decisions, not generic "best strategy" takes. The objective is to help you choose the option that survives real execution constraints and advisor review, then document that choice in an audit-defensible way.

Decision Framework (Execution-First)

Most failures happen because teams jump directly from idea to filing and skip method design. Run this sequence before implementation:

  1. Step 1: Define your primary objective first (cash flow, tax liability, liquidity, audit defensibility, or admin simplicity).
  2. Step 2: Validate qualification gates for both Solo 401(k) Loan and HELOC before comparing dollar outcomes.
  3. Step 3: Build a baseline model and at least two stress scenarios (conservative and adverse).
  4. Step 4: Score each option across decision factors, then pressure-test the top option against edge cases.
  5. Step 5: Select execution path and documentation standard before year-end, not during filing season.

Failure Modes and Mitigations

These are not hypothetical. They are the practical breakdowns that repeatedly turn a valid strategy into an expensive cleanup project:

Failure Mode Mitigation Control
Business income shock makes plan-loan repayment cadence difficult. Solo 401(k) Loan and HELOC should only be implemented after an explicit documentation standard is agreed with your advisor.
Rate spikes turn HELOC from tactical bridge into long-term cost drag. Replace assumptions with verifiable evidence (contracts, logs, policy docs, or third-party support).
Solo 401(k) Loan misuse: Income is highly unstable quarter to quarter. Use Solo 401(k) Loan only when the qualification gate is clearly met and documented before filing.
HELOC misuse: You cannot tolerate variable-rate uncertainty. Use HELOC only when the execution process can be maintained consistently during the year.

Edge Cases That Change the Decision

  • Business income shock makes plan-loan repayment cadence difficult.
  • Rate spikes turn HELOC from tactical bridge into long-term cost drag.
  • Borrower underestimates closing and reserve liquidity needs.
  • Down payment strategy ignores post-close capex requirements.

When Not to Use This Strategy

Avoid Solo 401(k) Loan if...

  • Income is highly unstable quarter to quarter.
  • You cannot sustain strict repayment cadence.
  • You are near retirement and cannot absorb sequence risk.

Avoid HELOC if...

  • You cannot tolerate variable-rate uncertainty.
  • Collateral position is fragile.
  • You need fixed repayment predictability.

  • Define exit/refinance trigger criteria before borrowing.

  • Document funding choice with risk controls and contingency steps.
  • Run post-implementation review, compare projected vs actual results, and adjust the playbook for next quarter.

Questions to Ask Your CPA/Advisor

  • What repayment stress threshold makes this financing unsafe?
  • How should we size liquidity reserves post-closing?
  • What rate scenario breaks HELOC viability for my deal?
  • What contingency plan should we pre-approve if income drops?

What to include in your advisor packet

  • A one-page objective memo clarifying what "winning" means for this decision (Solo 401(k) Loan vs HELOC).
  • Baseline and alternative math model with all assumptions clearly listed.
  • Supporting evidence folder for qualification, valuations, logs, and policy records.
  • Risk memo covering edge cases, red flags, and fallback plan if assumptions fail.
  • Annual review checklist showing what will be re-evaluated before next filing cycle.
