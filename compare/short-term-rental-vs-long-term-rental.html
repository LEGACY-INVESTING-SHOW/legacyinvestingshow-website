Short-term rental vs long-term rental

One is a small hotel. One is a lease. Tax rules follow the stay length and your hours, not the brand of the app.

A long-term rental is a home you lease for months at a time, often a year. A short-term rental (STR) is a stay measured in nights, often listed on Airbnb or Vrbo. STR usually means more guest work, more furniture, and more local rules. Tax is a separate test. IRS Publication 925 says an activity is not a 'rental activity' for passive-loss rules if the average stay is 7 days or less. You still have to materially participate. The 7-day test, in plain words

IRS Publication 925 says an activity is not treated as a rental activity if the average period of customer use is 7 days or less. You figure that average by dividing total days in all rental periods by the number of rentals for the year.

Clearing 7 days does not make losses deductible. You still need material participation, which is a separate hour-and-facts test in the same publication. A full-time W-2 job makes some of those tests hard. A spouse's hours can matter on a joint return. That is facts, not a loophole slogan.

Long-term residential leases usually stay in the rental-activity bucket. Losses are often passive unless you qualify as a real estate professional. That status needs more than 750 hours and more than half of your working time in real property trades. A full-time job usually blocks the second half. Details: REPS vs STR loophole.

How to compare dollars without a fantasy spreadsheet

Use two tools on the same property idea. Rental cash flow for a yearly lease. STR break-even occupancy for a nightly model. Do not copy someone else's occupancy. Use a conservative number you can defend.

Add cleaning, supplies, furniture replacement, and permit fees to the STR side. Add vacancy and repairs to both. Then ask whether you will do the work. A prettier STR net that needs you every weekend is not passive.

W-2 readers

Do not buy an STR because a class said it 'unlocks depreciation on wages.' Read the short-term rental loophole guide and talk to a CPA. Depreciation can reverse when you sell. See our recapture guide if you go that far.

Questions to ask before you pay

Get the answers in writing. A good seller can wait a day.

  • Is STR even legal on this property?
  • Who cleans, and what does that cost on a slow month?
  • What occupancy do I need to break even, written down?
  • If I am buying this for tax, have I read the 7-day test and material participation with a CPA?
  • What happens to my numbers if I have to switch to a 12-month lease?

Questions people ask

Is short-term always more profit?

No. Higher nightly rates can lose after cleaning, vacancy, and furniture. Run both models. We will not quote a typical occupancy because we do not have your city data.

Does a 7-day average stay cut my W-2 tax?

Not by itself. It can change whether the activity is a rental for passive-loss rules. You still need material participation and a real property. Ask a CPA.

Can I switch from STR to long-term mid year?

Operations yes, if the law allows. The average-stay math for that tax year can get messy if long stays mix with short stays. Publication 925 is the starting text. Then a CPA.
