## The Self-Custody Threshold: 1 BTC Security Upgrade

Justin's plan includes a **critical custody transition at 1 Bitcoin accumulation** — the point where exchange counterparty risk exceeds self-custody complexity.

### Exchange Custody Risks

**Historical Precedents:**
- Mt. Gox (2014): 850,000 BTC lost ($450M at the time)
- FTX (2022): $8B+ in customer funds misappropriated
- Celsius, BlockFi, Voyager (2022): Lending platform insolvencies
- **Not your keys, not your coins** — proven repeatedly

**Ongoing Risks:**
- Exchange hacks (security breaches)
- Regulatory freezes (government action)
- Insolvency (rehypothecation, leverage losses)
- Internal fraud (rogue employees)
- **Insurance**: Most exchanges carry minimal or no deposit insurance

### Self-Custody Implementation: 2-of-3 Multi-Sig

**Hardware Wallet Selection:**
- **Coldcard Mk4**: Bitcoin-only, air-gapped, open source ($157)
- **SeedSigner**: DIY, Bitcoin-only, ultra-secure ($50-100)
- **BitBox02**: Swiss-made, simple interface ($150)
- **Blockstream Jade**: Affordable, open source ($65)

**2-of-3 Multi-Signature Setup:**
1. **Key 1**: Justin's hardware wallet (primary spending)
2. **Key 2**: Bank safe deposit box backup (physical security)
3. **Key 3**: Trusted third party (attorney, family member, executor)

**Transaction Requirements:**
- Any 2 of 3 keys required to spend
- Theft of 1 key = funds safe
- Loss of 1 key = recoverable with remaining 2
- Geographic distribution reduces single-point-of-failure

### Custody Transition Process

**At 1 BTC (~$70,000-$100,000):**
1. Purchase Coldcard or SeedSigner hardware wallet
2. Generate seed phrase in isolated environment
3. Practice transactions with small amounts
4. Transfer 0.1 BTC test amount
5. Verify receipt and transaction signing
6. Transfer remaining balance in tranches
7. Distribute backup keys to secure locations

**Annual Security Review:**
- Verify hardware wallet functionality
- Check backup key accessibility
- Update firmware if needed
- Review inheritance planning documentation


## S-Corporation Structure: Tax Optimization for Side Income

While Justin's primary income is W-2, any side business or consulting income benefits from **S-Corporation structuring** — converting self-employment tax liability into distribution income.

### How S-Corp Tax Savings Work

**Side Income Example:**
- Consulting revenue: $50,000
- Business expenses: $10,000
- Net business income: $40,000

**Sole Proprietorship:**
- $40,000 × 15.3% self-employment tax = $6,120
- Plus ordinary income tax on full $40,000

**S-Corporation:**
- $20,000 reasonable salary (15.3% = $3,060 SE tax)
- $20,000 distribution (0% SE tax)
- **Tax savings: $3,060 annually**

**Larger Side Income ($150,000):**
- $70,000 salary ($10,710 SE tax)
- $80,000 distribution ($0 SE tax)
- **Tax savings: $12,240 annually**

### Reasonable Salary Determination

**IRS Requirements:**
- Must pay "reasonable compensation" for services
- Based on industry standards, experience, time commitment
- Can't be zero or unrealistically low

**Justin Documentation:**
- Industry salary surveys for consulting role
- Comparable W-2 positions in field
- Time tracking showing hours devoted
- Annual compensation review memo

**Conservative Approach:**
- Set salary at 50-60% of net business income
- Adjust annually based on hours/revenue
- Never zero, never unreasonably low
- **Audit defense**: Documented rationale

### Implementation Timeline

**Month 1: Entity Formation**
- Form LLC in state of residence
- File Form 2553 for S-Corp election
- Obtain EIN from IRS

**Month 2: Infrastructure Setup**
- Business bank account (separate from personal)
- Accounting system (QuickBooks or Wave)
- Payroll service (Gusto or ADP for single-person)

**Month 3: Operational Launch**
- Begin consulting engagements through S-Corp
- Invoicing and payment collection
- Expense tracking and categorization

**Year-End Optimization:**
- December salary adjustment based on annual income
- Maximize distribution vs. salary ratio (legally)
- Solo 401(k) contributions from salary portion
