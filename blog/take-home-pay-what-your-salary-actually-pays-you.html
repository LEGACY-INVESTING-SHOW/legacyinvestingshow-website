Take-Home Pay: What Your Salary Actually Pays You (2026)

A $100,000 salary takes home about $79,000 after federal tax and FICA. See the paycheck anatomy, the 2026 numbers, and how to change your take-home.

Preston Seo, August 9, 2026, 4 min read

The offer letter says $100,000. The paycheck says otherwise, and the gap is not a mystery — it is a list. Understanding the list is worth more than complaining about it, because every line on it is a decision you can change.

For a single filer with no state income tax, a $100,000 salary takes home about $79,000 a year — roughly $6,580 a month on 12 pays — after federal income tax of about $13,400 and FICA of $7,650. Add state tax, health benefits, and a 401(k), and the real number drops further. The gross-to-net pay calculator runs your exact payroll with the rates you enter.

The paycheck anatomy

Every paystub is the same stack:

  1. Gross pay — your salary or hourly earnings before anything.
  2. Federal income tax — withheld according to your W-4, not your tax bracket. Your bracket is decided at filing; withholding is your estimate.
  3. FICA — 7.65% split between Social Security (6.2%, up to the wage cap) and Medicare (1.45%, no cap, plus 0.9% above $200,000 for single filers).
  4. State and local tax — varies from zero (Texas, Florida) to over 13% top rates (California), and it is a real line on the paycheck.
  5. Your elections — health insurance, 401(k), HSA, FSA, and everything else you signed up for.

The 2026 federal numbers

  • Standard deduction: $16,100 single, $24,150 head of household, $32,200 married filing jointly. This is the floor every itemization decision measures against.
  • Rates: the federal brackets run 10% to 37%, and they are marginal — each bracket applies only to the income inside it.
  • FICA: 7.65% on wages, with the Social Security cap around $180,000 in 2026.

The 2026 updates are covered in the site's 2026 tax changes guide. What matters for take-home: the standard deduction is the reason a $100,000 salary pays about 13% effective federal income tax, not 22%.

The worked example

$100,000, single filer, no state tax:

Line Amount
Gross salary $100,000
Federal income tax −$13,400 (approx.)
FICA −$7,650
Take-home before benefits $78,950
Monthly (12 pays) $6,580
Biweekly (26 pays) $3,036

Now add the choices: a 6% 401(k) contribution ($6,000) costs about $4,700 of take-home — the tax deduction pays part of the bill. Health insurance at $300 a month is the full $3,600. State tax, where it exists, takes another slice. Run each line through the gross-to-net calculator with your real rates and benefits, and compare the result against your paystub — the difference between the two is usually a missing line, and finding it is the point.

The two ways to change the number

  • Pre-tax elections. A 401(k), HSA, and health premiums lower taxable income, so they cost less than their face value in take-home. An HSA is triple-taxed in your favor: pre-tax in, tax-free growth, tax-free out for medical costs.
  • Withholding accuracy. If your W-4 is wrong, the IRS is running a free loan on your money or handing you a bill in April. The W-2 withholding fix guide covers the mid-year correction, and the payroll withholding calculator prices the adjustment.

If your income is uneven — bonuses, side income, a business — the self-employment tax reserve calculator handles the quarterly side, and the tax calculators cover the rest of the payroll stack.

Bottom line

Take-home pay is gross minus a short list, and every line on the list is a decision. Know the standard deduction, watch the FICA line, and make your pre-tax elections deliberately. The gross-to-net pay calculator turns your salary into your real number in one pass.

Frequently asked questions

How much take-home pay do I get from a $100,000 salary?

A single filer in a no-income-tax state takes home roughly $79,000 a year — about $6,580 a month on 12 pays — after about $13,400 of federal income tax and $7,650 of FICA, before benefits and retirement contributions.

What is the difference between gross pay and net pay?

Gross pay is your salary before anything is withheld. Net pay (take-home) is what lands in your account after federal and state income tax, FICA, and your elected deductions like health insurance and 401(k).

How much is FICA tax in 2026?

FICA is 7.65% of wages: 6.2% Social Security plus 1.45% Medicare. The Social Security portion stops at the wage cap (about $180,000 in 2026); Medicare continues with an extra 0.9% above $200,000 for single filers.

Does a 401(k) reduce my take-home pay by the full contribution?

No. A $500 monthly 401(k) contribution reduces take-home by less than $500 because it lowers taxable income. In the 22% bracket it costs about $390 of take-home, with the other $110 staying in your pocket through lower withholding.

Why is my take-home different from a calculator's estimate?

The estimate usually excludes state tax, benefits, garnishments, and your exact W-4 settings. Run the calculator with your own rates and compare against your paystub line by line.
