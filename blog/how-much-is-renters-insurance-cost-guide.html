How Much Is Renters Insurance in 2026? Average Costs by State

Average renters insurance costs about $170 a year nationally. See what coverage includes, which states pay more, and how to price your own policy.

Use This Like a Tool

The point of this page is not more information. The point is better judgment before you act.

  • Pull the real numbers first.
  • Run a base case and a stress case.
  • Use the result to make a cleaner decision, not a faster emotional one.

Before you shop for renters insurance, decide what you are actually buying. The policy is not a bet on your apartment burning down. It is a liability policy with a small property rider attached, and it costs less than one parking ticket a month in most of the country.

The national average premium is about $170 a year — roughly $14 a month — based on the most complete state dataset available, the NAIC's HO-4 filings published through the Insurance Information Institute. Most renters pay between $100 and $300 a year. A kitchen fire, a guest injury, or a burglary claim routinely runs five figures, which is why the coverage is the product and the premium is the cost of carrying it.

What your $14 a month is actually buying

Every standard renters policy has the same four parts:

  • Personal property. Your furniture, clothes, electronics, and everything else you own, up to the limit you pick.
  • Personal liability. Defense costs and judgments if someone is hurt in your home or you damage someone else's property.
  • Loss of use. A hotel and meals if your unit becomes uninhabitable, say after a fire.
  • Medical payments to others. Small no-fault payments when a guest gets hurt, regardless of fault.

The gaps matter just as much. Flooding needs a separate FEMA policy. Earthquakes need an endorsement. Your roommate's laptop is not covered by your policy — they need their own. Jewelry and cameras carry small sub-limits, so expensive pieces need a scheduled rider. Pest damage and normal wear are never covered.

A Texan at the $250 average and an Idahoan at $103 should both expect their own policy to land near their state's band — and both should be suspicious of a quote far above it.

Why the number moves

Four state-level forces explain most of the gap between Texas and Idaho:

  1. Claim frequency and litigation — states with more theft, fire, and lawsuits price higher.
  2. Replacement cost of belongings — contents limits track local living costs.
  3. Weather exposure — coastal and storm-prone states carry more risk.
  4. Carrier competition — more insurers usually means lower averages.

Then your personal factors layer on: coverage limit, deductible, credit history where allowed, and whether you bundle with auto. That last one matters — the renters-plus-auto bundle usually beats buying either separately.

The two ways people misbuy

The mistake is rarely "I bought too much." It is almost always one of these:

  • Underinsuring contents. People pick a round number like $15,000 and never inventory what they own. A modest apartment's contents at replacement cost — furniture, electronics, kitchen, clothes, tools — usually land at $25,000-$50,000. The home contents value guide and its calculator fix this in one evening.
  • Buying the cheapest quote with no comparison. A $90-a-year policy with a $500 deductible and $10,000 contents limit is not cheaper — it is a different product. Compare identical limits and deductibles across three carriers.

How to price yours in ten minutes

  1. Enter your state, contents value, liability limit, and deductible in the renters insurance cost calculator. That gives you the number your quote should land near.
  2. Inventory your belongings once, using the home contents calculator, so the limit is real.
  3. Quote three carriers at identical coverage, and ask each for the bundle price with auto.
  4. Set the deductible where you can actually absorb a loss — $500 or $1,000 — and keep the savings in your emergency fund instead of the premium.

The same shopping discipline applies to the rest of your coverage. The auto insurance cost guide walks through the renewal trap most drivers accept, and the insurance calculators cover the deductible tradeoffs and coverage gaps across all your policies.

Bottom line

At $14 a month nationally, renters insurance is a rounding error in most budgets and the difference between a bad year and a ruined one after a claim. Price it with real limits, bundle it, and move on. The renters insurance calculator gives you the number in the time it takes to order coffee.
