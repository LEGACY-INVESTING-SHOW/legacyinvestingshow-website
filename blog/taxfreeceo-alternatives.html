How to choose

Step 1. Decide if you are buying knowledge or labor.

A course gives you knowledge. A firm gives you labor. They cost different amounts for good reasons. If you are busy and the money is already there, labor is often the better buy. If you want to understand your own return, buy knowledge first.

Step 2. Check what you own.

Most of the strategies these programs teach need one of two things: a business or a rental property. If you have neither, the honest list of moves is short. Max your 401(k) and HSA, use a taxable account well, and then decide whether you want to build a business or buy property. A program cannot skip that step for you.

Step 3. Fix the books before you buy strategy.

If you own a business and your bookkeeping is a mess, no strategy program helps yet. An accounting firm like LYFE Accounting solves that problem first. Strategy on top of bad records is guesswork.

Step 4. Ask who actually answers you.

Anderson Platinum publishes a weekday window for its Q&A room. Business Owner HQ publishes biweekly live calls. A course with a forum is not the same as a person who knows your file. Ask before you pay.

Step 5. Get the total in writing.

Enrollment fee, monthly fee, term length, renewal price, refund window. Five numbers. Ask for all five in an email. A seller who will not put them in writing has told you something.
