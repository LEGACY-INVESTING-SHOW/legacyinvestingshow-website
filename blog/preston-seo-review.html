Is Legacy Investing Show Legit?

Yes. It is a real registered company that sells paid courses and coaching. It is not a free service, and it is not cheap. Here is what you can check yourself, plus the complaints people make and what I say about each one.

What the company is

Legacy Investing Show, LLC is based in Herriman, Utah. The Better Business Bureau profile lists it under Real Estate School. The company is not BBB accredited.

You can also look at the two Trustpilot profiles. The main one is Legacy Investing Show By Preston Seo, with 66 reviews and a 4.2 score. There is a second, nearly empty profile for legacyinvestingshow.com with one review.

The four complaints people make

On Trustpilot, 77% of the 66 reviews are five star and 9% are one star. The one star reviews repeat four things.

1. Refunds. Reviewers say they asked for a refund and did not get a clear answer, or that the contract was not refundable.

What I say: this site does not publish refund terms. Our terms page says paid access is governed by the offer terms shown at checkout. So the terms live in the agreement you sign. Ask for that agreement in writing and read the refund section before you pay. If a rep will not send it, walk away.

2. Slow replies. Reviewers say email and Zoom support was slow or hard to reach.

What I say: Trustpilot shows we reply to 83% of negative reviews and usually take more than a month to do it. That is too slow, and I own it. Before you buy, ask who your point of contact is, which channel they use, and how fast they reply. Get the answer in writing.

3. The price moved during the sales call. Reviewers say the price dropped when they hesitated.

What I say: the list prices are above. Two things can change the number you hear. The Blueprint is $9,800 paid by cash or card, and $10,800 through Splitit financing, because financing costs money. We also run promotions at some points in the year. Neither is a reason to decide in one call. Ask for the price in writing and take it away with you.

4. The testimonials are all interviews I run myself. This one is true.

What I say: most of the video stories on this site are calls I recorded with students. That is useful, but it is not independent. So I also put the written student quotes, their names, and where each one came from on one page. See reviews and student results. Then read the third-party pages I linked above, which I do not control.

What to ask before you pay

  • Send me the enrollment agreement and show me the refund section.
  • What are the written terms of the No Client Left Behind Guarantee? (Our Legacy Wealth Blueprint page tells you to ask for these on the call, so ask.)
  • Who is my point of contact, and how fast do they reply?
  • Is this price the full price, and will you put it in writing?
  • Can I speak to two students who started where I am now?

Good answers to those five questions tell you more than any review page.

Growing Up: The Foundation

My parents immigrated from Korea with nothing.

Literally nothing. My dad worked three jobs. My mom cleaned houses. They slept in a garage for the first two years.

I watched them sacrifice everything so I could have opportunity.

That created two things in me:

  1. A deep respect for hard work
  2. A burning desire to build something that lasts

I didn't want my kids to see me struggle like I saw my parents struggle.

Corporate Years: Learning to Sell

Started in sales at 22.

Door-to-door at first. Worst job ever. But I learned to handle rejection.

Moved to B2B sales. Tech industry. Started making good money.

By 26 I was earning $150K/year.

Sounds great, right?

Here's the problem: I was still trading time for money. If I stopped working, the income stopped.

Plus, I hated having a boss. Hated office politics. Hated asking permission to take vacation.

The Real Estate Journey

Started investing at 24 while working full-time.

First deal: A duplex I house-hacked. Lived in one unit, rented the other.

That duplex cash-flowed $400/month. Covered most of my mortgage.

Lightbulb moment: Money can work for you. You don't have to work for money.

Over the next 4 years I bought 12 more properties. Mix of small multifamily and single-family rentals.

At 28 my real estate income exceeded my salary.

Quit my job. Never looked back.

The $20M Portfolio Breakdown

Current holdings:

  • 47 rental units across 12 properties
  • Mix of long-term rentals and short-term (Airbnb)
  • $20M+ in total value
  • $85K+/month in gross revenue
  • $42K+/month in net cash flow

Not bragging. Just proving I've done what I teach.

The Pivot to Education

Started posting on YouTube in 2019.

Just sharing what I was learning. The wins, the mistakes, the systems.

People started asking questions. Lots of questions.

Realized there was a gap in the market. Most real estate education is either:

  1. Motivational fluff with no substance
  2. Outdated theory from people who haven't invested in years

I wanted to teach what actually works right now. Based on real experience.

Legacy Investing Show was born.

My Teaching Philosophy

I believe three things:

1. Real estate is a business, not a magic button

You have to treat it seriously. Follow systems. Do the work.

Anyone promising passive income with no effort is lying.

2. Specificity wins

I don't teach "find good deals."

I teach: "Here's the exact script to message landlords. Here's the exact spreadsheet to analyze deals. Here's the exact furniture list."

Specificity creates results.

3. Student success is the only metric that matters

I could have the fanciest website. The best marketing. The most followers.

None of it matters if students don't get results.

Our success rate is high because we filter who we let in. We only work with people who will actually execute.

My Results vs. Student Results

Here's what I think separates legitimate educators from fake gurus:

Fake gurus: Only talk about their own results. Never show student wins.

Legitimate educators: Celebrate student results above their own.

Yes, I've built a $20M portfolio. That's table stakes. Proof I know what I'm talking about.

But I'm more proud of Marcus making $18K/month.

I'm more proud of Jennifer escaping the nursing grind.

I'm more proud of Sarah making almost her teaching salary from one unit.

Their results matter more than mine.

What I'm Not

Let me be clear about what I don't do:

I don't guarantee results. Business has risk. Some students don't execute. Some pick bad markets. Some give up too early.

I don't do the work for you. I give you the roadmap. You have to drive.

I'm not for everyone. If you need hand-holding and constant reassurance, I'm not your guy. I work with action-takers.

I won't sell you on hype. No Lamborghinis. No private jets. No "laptop lifestyle" photos on the beach.

Just real business. Real systems. Real results.

The Books That Shaped My Thinking

If you want to understand how I think, read these:

  • "Rich Dad Poor Dad" by Robert Kiyosaki (the starting point)
  • "The Millionaire Real Estate Investor" by Gary Keller (the framework)
  • "The Book on Rental Property Investing" by Brandon Turner (the tactics)
  • "The E-Myth Revisited" by Michael Gerber (systems thinking)

I've read each of these at least three times. Underlined. Highlighted. Notes in the margins.

My Daily Routine (When Students Ask)

People always want to know what I do day-to-day.

Here's the honest answer:

Morning: Wake up at 6 AM. Coffee. Check my Airbnb dashboards. Review guest messages. Handle any issues. Usually done by 8 AM.

Midday: Work on Legacy Investing Show. Create content. Coach students. Improve systems.

Afternoon: Property management stuff. Review applications. Approve maintenance requests. Check financials.

Evening: Family time. Dinner with my wife and kids. No work after 6 PM unless there's an emergency.

I work about 30 hours per week total. Used to work 60+. Systems changed everything.

Why I Teach Instead of Just Investing

Fair question. If I'm so successful, why teach?

Three reasons:

1. Impact Building wealth changed my life. Teaching others to do the same is deeply satisfying.

2. Challenges Investing is fun but gets repetitive after a while. Teaching keeps me sharp. Forces me to explain things clearly.

3. Business model The education business generates cash flow I can reinvest into more real estate. Flywheel effect.

I'm not apologizing for making money teaching. I provide massive value. Students get results.

How to Evaluate Me (or Any "Guru")

Before you trust anyone with your education, ask these questions:

Do they actually do what they teach? I still own and operate properties. I'm not teaching outdated theory.

Do they show student results? Look at our success stories. Real names. Real numbers. Real people.

Do they have skin in the game? I'm not a random marketer. I built this from zero. Still operate today.

Are they accessible? I'm in our community daily. Students message me directly. I respond.

Do they have haters? If someone has zero negative reviews, they're either new or hiding something. I've helped thousands of people. Some didn't get results. That's reality.
