Arbitrage, co-hosting, and ownership are three different jobs

People mix these up and then buy the wrong program.

Arbitrage means you sign a long-term lease and rent the place out by the night. Your money risk is the deposit, the furniture, and the rent you owe whether or not you get bookings. Your hardest step is getting a landlord to put permission to sublet in writing. No course can give you that permission.

Co-hosting means you run somebody else's listing and take a share of the revenue. Almost no cash up front. Your hard part is finding owners who will hire you and then keeping them happy.

Ownership means you buy the property. You need a down payment. You keep the equity and the tax benefits, and you carry the mortgage in a slow season.

BNB Formula and Airbnb Ascension are arbitrage programs. 10XBNB teaches arbitrage plus co-listing, which is its name for co-hosting.

About the sticker shock on coached programs

The most common question in Airbnb host forums is some version of this: why does a mentorship cost so much when the videos are on YouTube?

It is a fair question. The honest answer is that at that level you are not buying videos. You are buying people. Coaches who read your draft sublease, run your numbers before you sign, and act as a reference when a landlord asks who you are. If a coached program does not include that, it is overpriced.

The second question is also fair: is there proof it works for under $3,000?

Yes, in a limited way. Sean Rakidzich publishes exact prices for his self-paced courses, from $180 for BIG DATA up to $800 for the Closers Crash Course, all with a 30-day money-back guarantee. Buying one of those is a cheap way to find out whether the daily work of this business suits you. If cold calling landlords for three weeks makes you quit, you learned that for $180 instead of a coached program.

What a cheap course cannot do is review your specific lease in your specific city. That is the gap the expensive programs are supposed to fill.

How to choose

Start with legality, not with the course. Check your city's short-term rental rules first. If your target city bans stays under 30 days in the zones you can afford, no program helps. Read our guide on how to evaluate an Airbnb market before you spend anything.

Then check your capital. Arbitrage on one unit usually needs deposits, furniture, and a few months of rent in reserve. If a program fee would use up that reserve, buy the cheap course and keep your cash. The reserve is what keeps you alive through a slow first quarter.

Then pick on format, not on brand size. If you want to learn alone at night, a self-paced library works. If you freeze when a landlord pushes back, pay for a coach you can message the same day.

Then ask how the investment is structured before the call. Two of the three programs here will not give you a number until you are on the phone. We do not publish a checkout menu either, because the path depends on your situation and how you pay. That is a normal sales choice, but you are allowed to ask by email first.

Airbnb Ascension: the honest version

Airbnb Ascension is a coached program matched to your situation and goals, with custom options, payment plans, and financing so the first check is not what keeps you from changing cash flow. Term and scope are set on the call. How many properties you finish depends on your speed, your capital, your credit, the deals that pass review, and your local rules. "No cap" on a longer engagement is not a promise of a number.

What is included: a dedicated 1-on-1 coach with a 30 to 45 minute onboarding call and same-day messaging access, coaches who will act as a reference with landlords, property profitability reports on deals before you sign, a ranked market list, 7+ hours of self-paced curriculum with lifetime access, weekly live Zoom calls, landlord outreach and closing scripts, sublease templates, a furnishing playbook, listing and pricing setup, and an entity and business credit strategy session.

There is no published money-back guarantee.

Who Airbnb Ascension is not for:

  • People who want to test the idea cheaply. If you are not sure you want to do this, a coached program is the wrong first step. Buy a $200 to $800 course, call 20 landlords, and see how you feel.
  • People without a reserve. The guidance we give is $10,000 to $25,000 of working capital on top of the program fee. If the fee is your capital, wait.
  • People who want to own property. Arbitrage builds cash flow, not equity. If you want the asset and the tax benefits, look at buying instead.
  • People who want a refund guarantee. We do not publish one. If that is your deal-breaker, buy from someone who does.
  • People who cannot give it real hours in the first 60 days. Launch is front-loaded. After that it calms down. Before that it does not.

Frequently asked questions

How much does BNB Formula cost?

Brian Page does not publish a price on the BNB Formula pages we could open. His course page offers a free book and routes you to a call. Competitor pages quote different figures that disagree with each other, so ask his team for the price and the refund terms in writing.

How much does 10XBNB cost?

10XBNB does not publish a price. We checked its program details, FAQ, and reviews pages. The FAQ sends you to the contact page, so the number comes on a call.

How much does Airbnb Ascension cost?

Airbnb Ascension is a coached program matched to your situation and goals, with custom options, payment plans, and financing so the first check is not what keeps you from changing cash flow. Term and scope are set on the call, not off a public menu.

What is the difference between arbitrage, co-hosting, and ownership?

In arbitrage you lease a property and sublet it nightly, so you need landlord permission. In co-hosting you run someone else's listing for a share of revenue, so you need clients. In ownership you buy the property, so you need a down payment and you keep the equity.

Is there a cheaper way to test the model first?

Yes. Sean Rakidzich publishes exact prices for self-paced courses from $180 to $800 with a 30-day money-back guarantee. That is a low-cost way to see if the daily work suits you before you spend thousands.

Which of these publishes a money-back guarantee?

None of the three. Rakidzich publishes a 30-day money-back guarantee on his courses, which is why he is a useful benchmark. Always ask for refund terms in writing.

Why do coached arbitrage mentorships cost so much?

The fee usually pays for people, not videos: coaches who review your leases and numbers, and done-with-you setup work. Whether that is worth it depends on how much of that work you would otherwise do badly or not at all.
