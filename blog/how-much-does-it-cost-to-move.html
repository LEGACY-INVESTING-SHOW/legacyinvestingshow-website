How Much Does It Cost to Move? The Real 2026 Budget

How much does it cost to move: local moves run about $500-1,200 and long-distance $2,000-5,000+ (2025-26 estimates), plus the costs quotes never show.

Preston Seo

Use This Like a Tool

The point of this page is not more information. The point is better judgment before you act.

  • Pull the real numbers first.
  • Run a base case and a stress case.
  • Use the result to make a cleaner decision, not a faster emotional one.

The moving company prices a truck by the hour. The move prices itself by the box, the floor, the distance, and the date you pick — and the quote and the cost are rarely the same number.

This guide lays out the two budgets that matter (local and long-distance), what drives the price, and the costs the quote never shows.

Local versus long-distance: two different budgets

Move type Typical range What drives it
Local About $500-1,200 Crew hours, stairs and floors, truck size
Long-distance About $2,000-5,000+ Miles, weight, packing services, route

Both figures are 2025-26 industry estimates, and both are ranges on purpose. A studio down the block with no stairs lands near the bottom of the local band; a four-bedroom house across the country lands well above the top of the long-distance one.

Within the local band, the driver is crew time. Two movers for four hours on a flat first floor is a different quote from the same crew carrying a sectional up two flights. Within the long-distance band, weight dominates: a one-bedroom apartment travels for far less than a houseful of furniture, which is why the same route can quote $2,000 or $7,000 depending on the load.

DIY versus full-service: what the price includes

Option What you pay for What you provide
DIY rental truck Truck, fuel, dolly Packing, loading, driving, unloading
Hybrid Truck and two movers Packing and driving
Full-service Packing, loading, transport, unloading, insurance Your schedule

DIY looks cheapest on paper and usually is in cash. Full-service buys your time, your back, and the coverage on the piano in the stairs. Pick the option that matches your budget and your risk tolerance — then price it, because the gap is wide enough to change the decision.

The time math matters too. A full household takes most people two to three days to pack around their normal lives, plus a full day of loading and unloading. If your work schedule cannot absorb that, the full-service quote buys days, not just hours — and the insurance that comes with it covers the damage you would otherwise claim against your own deposit.

Packing is also a cost even when you do it yourself: boxes, tape, wrap, and padding run into the hundreds for a full house, and the cheapest boxes are the ones you collect weeks early rather than buy on moving week.

Seven drivers that change the quote

  • Distance and route. Miles matter less than hours for local moves, and everything for long-distance.
  • Weight and volume. Movers quote on the load, not the home.
  • Floors, stairs, and elevators. Third-floor walkups cost more, always.
  • The date. Month-end and summer weekends are peak; off-peak dates price lower.
  • Packing services. Full packing can rival the transport cost.
  • Storage. A gap between closing dates means a storage bill.
  • Insurance and valuation coverage. Basic coverage is included; real protection costs extra.

None of these drivers is optional. A quote that ignores stairs, or a phone estimate that never asks about the piano, is a quote built to be revised — usually upward, on move day, when you have no leverage.

The costs the quote never shows

  • Boxes, tape, and padding — hundreds of dollars for a full house.
  • Security deposits and first-month rent at the new place, plus any lease overlap you eat.
  • Utility hookups and deposits.
  • Time off work and the meals on the road.
  • A renters policy for the new address — the renters insurance guide prices it, and it belongs in the moving budget, not after it.

Build the budget in four buckets: the mover, the supplies, the month-of cash (deposits, utilities, lease overlap), and the after-move costs (insurance, the small furniture the new place lacks, the takeout on move-in day). The calculator below does exactly this — run it with your worst-case date, not your hopeful one.

Then add the safety margin: moves run late and apartments need deposits in the same week, so a buffer of 10-15% over the calculator total (practice guideline) is the difference between a tight move and a broken one.

How to get an estimate you can trust

Collect three quotes. Insist on an in-home or live video survey — a phone quote is a guess with a logo. Ask whether the price is binding or non-binding, and what changes it. Check the mover's license and complaint record before you put a deposit down.

Binding means the price holds once the survey is done; non-binding means the final number can move with the actual weight and hours. A reputable mover surveys properly and quotes binding — treat any resistance to the survey as the signal it is.

Ask about the valuation coverage in writing. Default coverage is often a few cents per pound (industry practice), which makes a $2,000 sofa worth $60 if it is damaged. Upgraded valuation is one of the cheapest line items on the invoice and the one people skip most.

Timing the quotes matters too: estimates are valid for a limited window, so collect them within the same two weeks and book once the survey is done. A quote that is three months old is a number with a birthday, not a price.

Build the budget before the truck arrives

Finally, choose the date with the market, not the calendar. Off-peak moves cost less and book more easily, and the savings from a midweek date can cover the renters policy for the new address.
