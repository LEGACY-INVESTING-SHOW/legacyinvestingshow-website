Salary to Hourly Pay: How to Convert Your Income (2026)

A $100,000 salary equals $48.08 an hour on the standard 2,080-hour year. See the formula, the overtime math, and how to compare salary and hourly offers.

Preston Seo, August 9, 2026, 5 min read

The recruiter says $100,000. The other offer says $48 an hour. Before you can compare them, you have to do the division, and the division changes how you see both offers.

$100,000 a year is $48.08 an hour, computed on the standard 2,080-hour year: 40 hours times 52 weeks. The salary to hourly calculator does the conversion for any number; this guide covers the assumptions hiding inside it.

The formula

Hourly rate = annual salary divided by 2,080 hours.

Computed examples:

Annual salary Hourly rate (2,080 hours)
$60,000 $28.85
$80,000 $38.46
$100,000 $48.08
$150,000 $72.12

(computed)

Reverse it for hourly offers: annual salary = hourly rate times 2,080.

What 2,080 assumes

The convention assumes full-time work, every week, with no unpaid time. It ignores three things:

  • Paid time off. Two weeks of vacation means about 2,000 hours of actual work, which makes the real rate higher: about $50 an hour on a $100,000 salary (computed).
  • Holidays. Ten paid holidays drop working hours to about 1,960, pushing the rate to about $51 (computed).
  • Unpaid hours. Salaried roles with steady unrecorded overtime make the effective rate worse than the conversion suggests. $100,000 divided by 2,600 hours is about $38.46 (computed).

The conversion is a comparison tool, so apply the same hour count to both sides of the comparison.

Part-time annualization works the same way. A $30-an-hour part-time role at 20 hours a week annualizes to $31,200 (computed: $30 times 1,040 hours), and the conversion is what lets you compare it to a full-time offer honestly.

Keep the same convention when the role changes. Converting a salaried promotion into hourly terms uses the same 2,080 denominator, and the comparison stays honest as long as the hour count does not move mid-negotiation.

Overtime math

Non-exempt hourly workers earn 1.5 times their regular rate after 40 hours in a week under federal law:

Regular rate Overtime rate (1.5x)
$20.00 $30.00
$30.00 $45.00
$48.08 $72.12

(computed)

A $48.08-an-hour job with five hours of overtime a week adds about $360 before taxes each week, or roughly $18,750 a year if the schedule holds (computed). Salaried employees are typically exempt from overtime, which is one of the real tradeoffs between the two pay structures.

Salary vs hourly: the tradeoffs

Factor Salary Hourly
Pay stability Fixed regardless of hours Varies with hours worked
Overtime Usually none 1.5x after 40 hours
Time off Paid, set by policy Unpaid if you do not work
Income volatility Predictable Fluctuates with the schedule
Loan qualification Steady income history Averaged or discounted

Lenders treat hourly income differently from salaried income, and the debt-to-income ratio calculator is where that difference shows up, since DTI runs on gross monthly income and lenders average variable pay.

Exempt and non-exempt status

The overtime question is decided by classification, not by title. Exempt employees are paid a salary and do not receive overtime for extra hours. Non-exempt employees are paid for every hour and receive the 1.5x premium past 40 hours. The same job title can sit on either side depending on duties and pay level, and the classification is worth checking before you accept an offer built on overtime expectations.

Reading an offer with the conversion

The conversion earns its keep at offer time. A $40-an-hour offer annualizes to $83,200 on 2,080 hours (computed), and ten hours of overtime a week at time and a half pushes it to about $114,000: $1,600 of regular pay plus $600 of overtime per week, times 52 (computed). Against a $100,000 salary with no overtime, the hourly offer wins on gross pay and loses on stability. The same comparison in reverse explains the salaried employee working 50-hour weeks: the $100,000 salary divided by 2,600 real hours is about $38.46, and the hourly offer starts to look better.

Benefits are part of the rate

Salary offers usually bundle benefits that hourly comparisons ignore: health insurance, retirement match, and paid leave. The health insurance comparison guide prices the coverage side; a family plan worth $15,000 a year adds that much to the salary side before the hourly math starts (computed example). Add the value of the benefits to the salary, then divide.

Pricing your time outside the job

The hourly conversion is also the negotiation tool for side work and freelance pricing. A $100,000 salary implies $48.08 an hour; charging less than that for freelance hours means the side work earns less than the main work, before considering the self-employment taxes and overhead a paycheck already covers. The conversion also works in reverse for jobs with commission or bonus potential: divide the expected total by 2,080 and compare the result against the guaranteed hourly rate.

Gross to net

The conversion is gross, and the paycheck is net. Federal and state tax, FICA, and benefits come out of both structures, and the take-home pay guide walks through the full anatomy of the paycheck. The difference between $48.08 an hour and what lands in the account is the part you can actually spend.

Hourly workers also face income volatility that salaried workers do not, which is why the emergency fund guide recommends a larger cash floor when hours vary.

Bottom line

Convert both offers on the same hour count, price overtime into hourly offers, and remember the conversion is gross. The salary to hourly calculator does the division, and the investing calculators cover the rest of the pay stack.

Frequently asked questions

How much is $100,000 a year per hour?

About $48.08, computed: $100,000 divided by 2,080 hours. On a 1,920-hour year that accounts for two weeks of vacation, it is about $52.08.

Why is the 2,080-hour year standard?

It is 40 hours times 52 weeks, the full-time convention used for comparisons. It overstates hours worked and ignores paid time off, so use the same hour count on both sides of any comparison.

How does overtime work for salaried employees?

Most salaried employees are exempt from overtime under the FLSA. Non-exempt hourly workers earn 1.5 times their regular rate after 40 hours in a week.

What is the formula for converting hourly to salary?

Multiply the hourly rate by 2,080 for the annual figure, before taxes. Reverse it with division for the hourly figure. The salary-to-hourly calculator does both.

Why does my take-home differ from the hourly math?

The conversion is gross. Federal and state tax, FICA, and benefits come out of both pay structures, and the take-home pay guide walks through the full difference.
