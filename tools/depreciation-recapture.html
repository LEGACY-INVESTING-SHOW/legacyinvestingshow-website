What this calculates

Split a rental sale into depreciation recapture and leftover long-term gain using basis, accumulated depreciation, and your rates.

How to use it

  1. Pull accumulated depreciation from prior returns or depreciation schedules.
  2. Use 25% unless your CPA tells you a different mix of 1245 and 1250.
  3. Compare this result to a 1031 if you are not done owning rentals.

Common mistakes

  • Reporting the whole gain at 15%.
  • Forgetting selling costs.
  • Ignoring NIIT on high MAGI years.

Formula

Gain = amount realized − (original basis − accumulated depreciation). Recapture = min(gain, accumulated depreciation).

FAQ

Is recapture ordinary income?+

Unrecaptured 1250 on real property is generally capped at 25%. Personal property (1245) can be ordinary. This tool uses the rate you enter for the recapture slice.

Does a 1031 avoid recapture now?+

A valid exchange can defer it. Recapture can still matter later.

Where is NIIT?+

Not included. Add 3.8% mentally if MAGI is over the NIIT threshold.

How the depreciation recapture calculator works

Splits a rental sale into the slice taxed as depreciation recapture and the slice taxed as long-term capital gain, using the two rates you enter.

Method

Adjusted basis at sale = original depreciable basis − accumulated depreciation, floored at zero.

Amount realized = sale price − selling costs. Total gain = amount realized − adjusted basis.

Depreciation recapture amount = the smaller of total gain and accumulated depreciation, never below zero.

Remaining gain at the long-term rate = total gain − recapture amount.

Estimated federal tax = recapture amount × your recapture rate + remaining gain × your long-term capital gains rate. This is the headline.

Assumptions

  • Default recapture rate is 25%, the maximum rate on unrecaptured Section 1250 gain from the building. Personal-property components from a cost segregation study are Section 1245 property recaptured at ordinary rates; blend your rate up if that describes you.
  • Default long-term rate is 15%. Depending on taxable income the federal long-term rate is 0%, 15%, or 20%; enter yours.
  • Federal only. No state tax and no 3.8% net investment income tax. Add NIIT mentally if your modified AGI is over the threshold in the sale year.
  • The property was held more than one year and sold outright: no installment sale, no 1031 exchange, no primary-residence exclusion.

Limitations

  • The tool uses one flat recapture rate. A property with both building depreciation and cost-segregated components has two recapture regimes, and it does not split them for you.
  • Suspended passive losses released on a fully taxable sale are not netted against the gain here. They can materially cut the real bill.
  • Original basis should include capitalized improvements and purchase costs; entering the bare purchase price overstates gain.

Frequently asked questions

Is depreciation recapture taxed as ordinary income?+

It depends on the asset. Depreciation on the building itself is unrecaptured Section 1250 gain, capped at 25%. Depreciation on 5-, 7-, and 15-year components from a cost segregation study is Section 1245 recapture, taxed at ordinary rates. This tool applies one rate you choose to the whole recapture slice.

Do I pay recapture on an Airbnb I ran under the short-term rental loophole?+

Yes. The depreciation that offset your W-2 income reduced your basis, and it comes back as gain on sale. The building share comes back at up to 25% and cost-seg components mostly at ordinary rates. The loophole changes when you get the deduction, not whether it is recaptured.

Can a 1031 exchange avoid depreciation recapture?+

A valid exchange defers it along with the rest of the gain. The deferred amount reduces your basis in the replacement property, so recapture resurfaces on a later taxable sale unless you exchange again or hold the property until death.

Does depreciation recapture apply if I never claimed depreciation?+

Generally yes. Recapture is based on depreciation allowed or allowable, so skipping the deduction does not avoid it. If you missed depreciation, ask a CPA about a Form 3115 accounting-method change to catch up before you sell.

Why is my total gain higher than the price appreciation?+

Because depreciation lowered your adjusted basis. Selling for exactly what you paid still produces gain equal to the depreciation you took, and that gain is the recapture slice.
