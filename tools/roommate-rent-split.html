What this calculates

Splits rent by room size after peeling off amenity premiums for things like parking, an ensuite, or a private balcony, so the split reflects what each person actually gets.

How to use it

  1. Start with total rent and work down the form, or load an example to begin from a realistic case.
  2. Read the headline result alongside the supporting rows, which show the intermediate figures behind it.
  3. Check the assumptions. They decide what the number includes and, more importantly, what it leaves out.
  4. Sweep total rent on the response curve to see how much it actually moves the answer.
  5. Run a cautious case as well as an optimistic one before using the estimate in a decision.

Common mistakes

  • Splitting rent evenly when one room is dramatically larger.
  • Renegotiating the split every time rent changes instead of agreeing on the formula once.
  • Forgetting that everyone on the lease is usually liable for the whole rent, whatever the split says.

Formula

Base = rent − total amenity premiums; each person pays base × their square footage share + their own premium

FAQ

What if there are only two roommates?+

Set the third roommate's square footage and amenity premium to zero and they drop out of the split.

How do I price an amenity premium?+

Ask what each person would pay to have it rather than not. A private bathroom often lands between $75 and $200 a month depending on the market. The number only has to feel fair to the people paying it.

Should square footage really drive the split?+

It is the most objective input available, which is why it works as a default. Natural light, noise, and closet space matter too, and the amenity premium is where you account for them.

Are these numbers financial advice?+

No. They are educational estimates based on the inputs and assumptions on this page. Confirm important decisions with a qualified professional and your own documents.
