What this calculates

Turn monthly rent, vacancy, ops, and debt service into annual cash flow divided by cash invested.

How to use it

  1. Cash invested is down payment plus closing plus initial capex.
  2. If CoC looks spectacular, the denominator is probably too small or ops are incomplete.
  3. Compare with cap rate to see how much of the return is leverage.

Common mistakes

  • Using purchase price instead of cash in.
  • Zero vacancy forever.
  • Leaving management out because you will self-manage.

Formula

Annual CF = (rent × (1 − vacancy%) − ops − debt) × 12. CoC = annual CF ÷ cash invested.

FAQ

Why is CoC higher than cap rate?+

Leverage. It can also go negative faster when rates rise.

Is 12% CoC good?+

Only if the cash flow survives a vacancy and a repair year. Percentage without a stress test is a slogan.

Does this work for arbitrage?+

Yes if cash in is deposits and furnishings. The Airbnb ROI tool is the fuller version.

How the cash-on-cash return calculator works

Annual pre-tax cash flow after vacancy, operating costs, and debt service, divided by the cash you actually invested.

Method

Effective monthly rent = gross monthly rent × (1 − vacancy and credit loss %).

Monthly cash flow = effective rent − monthly operating costs − monthly debt service.

Annual cash flow = monthly cash flow × 12.

Cash-on-cash return = annual cash flow ÷ cash invested. This is the headline.

Assumptions

  • Cash invested is down payment, closing costs, and any rehab or furnishing you paid for, not the purchase price.
  • Operating costs exclude the mortgage, which has its own field, and should include taxes, insurance, management, maintenance, and a capital reserve if you want an honest number.
  • Pre-tax. Depreciation, income tax, and appreciation are excluded.

Limitations

  • A single year at a single vacancy rate. It does not model a turnover year, a roof, or rent growth.
  • Leverage inflates the percentage in both directions: the same property with a bigger loan shows a higher return and a thinner cushion.
  • Tax effects that matter to high earners, such as depreciation losses offsetting W-2 income under the short-term rental rules or real estate professional status, are outside this metric.
  • Not investment advice.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good cash-on-cash return on a rental property?+

There is no universal number; it depends on financing, market, and how much risk you are taking. A return that only exists at zero vacancy and no repairs is a slogan. Re-run it with a realistic vacancy and a maintenance reserve and judge the survivable version.

Cash-on-cash vs cap rate: which one should I use?+

Cap rate measures the property without financing (NOI ÷ price) and is for comparing deals; cash-on-cash measures your financed return on the cash you put in. Use both: a decent cap rate with a bad loan produces a bad cash-on-cash.

Does cash-on-cash return include tax savings?+

No. It is pre-tax cash flow. Depreciation and bonus depreciation can change the after-tax result significantly, especially for a short-term rental where a material participant can use the loss against W-2 income. Model taxes separately.

Can I use this for an Airbnb or arbitrage deal?+

Yes. Enter expected monthly revenue after platform fees as rent, a realistic occupancy shortfall as vacancy, and deposits plus furnishing as cash invested. The Airbnb arbitrage ROI calculator does the fuller version.
