What this calculates

Convert monthly STR costs and ADR into the occupancy rate that covers the operation after platform fees.

How to use it

  1. Put every cost that does not vanish on a vacant night into monthly costs.
  2. Use trailing ADR, not a peak-weekend screenshot.
  3. If break-even occupancy is above realistic market occupancy, the deal is too tight.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving rent out of costs on an arbitrage deal.
  • Using 100% occupancy as the plan.
  • Adding cleaning fees into ADR.

Formula

Nights needed = monthly costs ÷ (ADR × (1 − fee%)). Occupancy = nights needed ÷ (365/12).

FAQ

That tool is a full cash-on-cash model. This one answers the single occupancy question operators actually argue about.

What occupancy is realistic?+

It is market-specific. If you cannot defend it with comps, do not underwrite it.

Should I include capex?+

If furniture and appliances will be replaced, add a monthly reserve to costs.

How the STR break-even occupancy calculator works

Solves for the single number STR operators argue about: the occupancy at which a listing stops losing money at your nightly rate.

Method

Net per occupied night = average nightly rate × (1 − platform and merchant fee %).

Occupied nights needed per month = fixed monthly costs ÷ net per occupied night.

Break-even occupancy = nights needed ÷ average days in a month (365 ÷ 12). This is the headline.

Assumptions

  • Fixed monthly costs include the lease or mortgage payment plus utilities, software, insurance, supplies, and any reserve you actually pay each month.
  • Guest-paid cleaning fees pass straight through to the cleaner and are excluded from both nightly rate and costs.
  • One nightly rate all year; no seasonality curve.

Limitations

  • It does not include variable costs that scale with bookings (extra utilities, consumables, per-booking management fees), so true break-even is slightly higher than shown.
  • Owner stays and blocked dates reduce available nights without changing the answer here.
  • Not investment advice.

Frequently asked questions

What is a realistic Airbnb occupancy rate?+

It is market and season specific. Pull comparable listings' trailing occupancy for your bedroom count and neighborhood, then compare that to this break-even figure. If comps run close to your break-even, the deal has no margin.

Should I count the mortgage or lease in fixed costs?+

Yes. For arbitrage the lease is the biggest cost; for an owned STR use the full mortgage payment including escrow. Leaving it out turns break-even into a fantasy.

Where do cleaning fees go?+

If guests pay a cleaning fee that covers the cleaner, leave both out. If you charge less than the cleaner costs, put the shortfall per turnover into monthly costs.

How does this relate to the short term rental loophole?+

It does not. Break-even is an operating metric. The tax side, where a short-term rental with an average stay of seven days or less and material participation can offset W-2 income with depreciation losses, is covered in the STR purchase analysis and the strategy guide.
