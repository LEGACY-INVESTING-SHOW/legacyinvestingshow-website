What this calculates

Compare Roth and Traditional future after-tax values for the same contribution and for the same paycheck cost.

How to use it

  1. Use current marginal rate, not effective rate.
  2. If you expect lower brackets in retirement, Traditional often wins on the same dollar.
  3. Read the same-paycheck row if Roth contributions come from after-tax cash.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing a $23k Roth with a $23k Traditional as equal paycheck cost.
  • Ignoring a match, which is usually pretax.
  • Using average tax rate in retirement for a large conversion year.

Formula

Traditional after-tax FV = FV × (1 − retirement rate). Same-paycheck Roth invests contribution × (1 − current rate).

FAQ

When is Roth obviously better?+

When you expect a higher rate later, or you want tax-free flexibility and are paying tax from cash you would not have invested anyway.

What about backdoor Roth?+

This is the growth comparison, not the pro-rata test.

Should high-income W-2s always pick Traditional?+

Often for the deduction, then convert in lower-income years. It is a sequence, not a forever choice.
