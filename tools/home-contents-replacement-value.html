What this calculates

Adds category-level replacement estimates so a renters or contents limit is grounded in an inventory rather than in a round number that felt about right.

How to use it

  1. Start with furniture and work down the form, or load an example to begin from a realistic case.
  2. Read the headline result alongside the supporting rows, which show the intermediate figures behind it.
  3. Check the assumptions. They decide what the number includes and, more importantly, what it leaves out.
  4. Sweep electronics on the response curve to see how much it actually moves the answer.
  5. Run a cautious case as well as an optimistic one before using the estimate in a decision.

Common mistakes

  • Setting coverage to a round number with no inventory behind it.
  • Valuing items at what you could sell them for rather than what replacing them costs.
  • Keeping the only copy of the inventory in the home it documents.

FAQ

Actual cash value or replacement cost?+

Enter the basis your policy uses. Replacement cost pays what a new equivalent costs today; actual cash value pays that minus depreciation, which on a five-year-old sofa can be most of the value.

Are valuables covered in full?+

Usually not. Standard policies apply sub-limits to jewellery, watches, art, collectibles, and cash that are far below the overall contents limit. Those items generally need scheduling separately.

Why does my total look so high?+

Because replacing everything at once is very different from what you paid over a decade. That is exactly the number a total loss would require, which is why the room-by-room walk matters.

How do I estimate the value of my home contents?+

Walk room by room and record what a new equivalent would cost today, not what you paid or could sell it for. The category totals here give you a defensible starting point for a policy limit.

Does renters insurance cover my laptop and phone?+

Yes, up to the policy's contents limit, though some policies cap payouts on individual items or require scheduling for high-value electronics. Check the sub-limits and deductible that apply.

How often should I update my contents inventory?+

At least once a year, and after any major purchase or move. Keep the list and photos outside the home, in cloud storage, so they survive the loss they document.

Are these numbers financial advice?+

No. They are educational estimates based on the inputs and assumptions on this page. Confirm important decisions with a qualified professional and your own documents.
