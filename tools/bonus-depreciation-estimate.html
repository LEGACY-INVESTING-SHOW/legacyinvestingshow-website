What this calculates

Turn qualifying short-life rental or business basis into an estimated bonus depreciation deduction and tax savings.

How to use it

  1. Use the personal-property or improvement basis, not the whole building.
  2. Set bonus to the rate that applies to the placed-in-service year.
  3. If you cannot use the loss this year, lower the tax-rate assumption.

Common mistakes

  • Applying bonus to land or 27.5-year residential rental structure.
  • Ignoring that unused losses may be suspended.
  • Forgetting recapture on sale or conversion.

Formula

Bonus = qualifying basis × bonus%. Tax savings = bonus × tax rate.

FAQ

Is furniture for an Airbnb bonus property?+

Often yes if it is personal property used in the business and placed in service. Keep invoices.

Does bonus replace a cost-seg study?+

No. Bonus needs a qualifying asset class. The study is how you support moving building costs into those classes.

What if my bonus year is not 100%?+

Enter the percentage that applies to your placed-in-service date.

How the bonus depreciation estimate works

Isolates one line of a depreciation plan: what a bonus percentage does to basis that already qualifies, and what that deduction is worth at your marginal rate.

Method

Bonus depreciation taken = qualifying basis × bonus percentage.

Estimated year-one tax savings = bonus depreciation × your combined marginal tax rate. This is the headline.

Remaining basis after bonus = qualifying basis − bonus depreciation. That remainder keeps depreciating over the asset's normal recovery period, which the tool does not schedule.

Assumptions

  • The basis you enter already qualifies: tangible property with a recovery period of 20 years or less (5-, 7-, or 15-year classes), qualified improvement property, or off-the-shelf software. The 27.5- or 39-year building shell and land do not qualify.
  • Tax year 2026 default is 100% bonus for qualified property acquired after January 19, 2025 and placed in service in 2026. Property under a written binding contract on or before January 19, 2025 stays on the old phase-down and gets 20% in 2026.
  • Marginal rate is whatever you enter. No state tax and no net investment income tax are added.
  • The property is placed in service in the year you are modeling. Bought but not yet in service means no deduction yet.

Limitations

  • No taxable-income, passive-activity, at-risk, or Section 461(l) excess business loss limits are applied. A big bonus deduction can create a loss you cannot use this year.
  • It does not compare bonus against Section 179 expensing, which has its own dollar limits and a business-income cap, or against electing out of bonus.
  • Recapture on sale or on conversion to personal use is ignored. Bonus on 5-, 7-, and 15-year property is mostly Section 1245 recapture at ordinary rates.
  • Not tax advice. Federal rules only.

Frequently asked questions

Does bonus depreciation apply in 2026?+

Yes, at 100% for qualified property acquired after January 19, 2025, which the 2025 tax law made permanent in Section 168(k). If your purchase was under a binding contract on or before January 19, 2025 it stays on the old phase-down at 20% for 2026, so enter 20 instead of 100.

Can I take bonus depreciation on a whole rental property?+

No. Residential rental buildings depreciate over 27.5 years and do not qualify. Bonus applies to the short-life components, which is why investors pair it with a cost segregation study that separates those components from the shell.

Is Airbnb furniture eligible for bonus depreciation?+

Generally yes. Furniture, appliances, and similar personal property used in the rental business are 5- or 7-year assets, so they qualify once placed in service. Keep invoices and the in-service date.

Can bonus depreciation offset W-2 income?+

Only when the loss is non-passive. For a long-term rental that usually means real estate professional status. For a short-term rental with an average guest stay of seven days or less, material participation (commonly more than 100 hours and more than anyone else) makes the loss non-passive without REPS. Otherwise the loss is suspended and carries forward.

What is the catch with a 100% deduction?+

You are pulling deductions forward, not creating them. Later years have less depreciation, and the deducted basis is recaptured when you sell, largely at ordinary rates for Section 1245 property. The benefit is timing and rate arbitrage, so model the exit too.
