What this calculates

Estimate Section 199A QBI as the lesser of 20% of QBI, 20% of taxable income, and optionally 50% of W-2 wages.

How to use it

  1. Use profit after ordinary business deductions, not gross receipts.
  2. If you are well under the threshold, leave the wage limit off.
  3. If you are over it, turn the limit on and see whether wages or QBI binds.

Common mistakes

  • Treating W-2 wages paid to yourself in an S corp as increasing QBI. They reduce QBI.
  • Ignoring SSTB phaseouts for consultants and similar fields.
  • Using AGI instead of taxable income.

Formula

QBI deduction ≈ min(20% × QBI, 20% × taxable income, and if toggled 50% × W-2 wages).

FAQ

Does an S corp help QBI?+

Sometimes. Reasonable salary creates wages that can support the limitation, but salary itself is not QBI. Model both.

Are rental STRs QBI?+

They can be if they rise to a trade or business. That is facts-and-circumstances. Do not assume it.

Is 20% automatic?+

No. Taxable income and wage/property tests can cut it. This is the simple screen.

How the QBI deduction estimate works

A simple Section 199A screen: the 20% deduction capped by taxable income and, if you turn it on, by 50% of W-2 wages.

Method

Compute three figures: 20% of qualified business income, 20% of taxable income before the QBI deduction, and 50% of the W-2 wages the business pays.

Deduction = the smaller of 20% of QBI and 20% of taxable income.

If the wage limit is switched on (enter 1), the deduction is further capped at 50% of W-2 wages.

The rows show each of the three figures so you can see which one is binding.

Assumptions

  • The income is qualified business income from a domestic trade or business, and the W-2 wages are wages that business paid, including reasonable salary paid to an owner of an S corporation.
  • Taxable income means taxable income before the QBI deduction, not AGI.
  • The wage limit applies only above the taxable-income threshold; the tool leaves it to you to turn it on.
  • Federal only, tax year 2026 rules; the 20% rate is used as written in Section 199A.

Limitations

  • Specified service trade or business (SSTB) phase-outs for consultants, health, law, financial services, and similar fields are not modeled; above the threshold those taxpayers can lose the deduction entirely.
  • The alternative wage-and-property test (25% of wages plus 2.5% of the unadjusted basis of qualified property) is not modeled; it can rescue wage-light real estate businesses.
  • Losses from other businesses, REIT dividends, and publicly traded partnership income are ignored.
  • Not tax advice.

Frequently asked questions

Does rental income qualify for the QBI deduction?+

It can when the rental activity rises to a trade or business or meets the IRS safe harbor for rental real estate enterprises. Triple-net leases and casual rentals often do not. Short-term rentals run like a hospitality business are stronger candidates. Do not assume it.

Does an S corp help or hurt QBI?+

Both. Reasonable salary paid to you counts as W-2 wages that support the limitation above the threshold, but that salary reduces QBI. Run this screen with and without the wage limit and compare against the S corp payroll tax savings.

Is the QBI deduction 20% of my profit?+

Only when 20% of taxable income is higher and no wage or SSTB limit bites. Taxable income is usually the binding figure for households with modest other income and large deductions.

Do W-2 wages I earn from an employer count?+

No. Only wages paid by the qualified business count for the wage limit, and your employer wages are not QBI. This tool asks for the business's wages.
