What this calculates

Compare sole-proprietor SE tax with employer-plus-employee FICA on a reasonable S corp salary using 2026 Social Security wage-base math.

How to use it

  1. Use profit after ordinary expenses, before owner draws.
  2. Set salary to something you can defend with role, hours, and comps.
  3. If savings are smaller than payroll friction, stay on Schedule C.

Common mistakes

  • Setting salary near zero.
  • Forgetting employer FICA is a real check you write.
  • Ignoring QBI wage effects.

Formula

Sole-prop SE ≈ 15.3% of 92.35% of profit (SS capped). S corp FICA ≈ 15.3% of salary (SS capped). Savings = SE − FICA.

How the S corp vs sole prop calculator works

Isolates payroll tax: self-employment tax on Schedule C profit versus employer-plus-employee FICA on an S corporation salary, using 2026 wage-base math.

Method

Sole-proprietor self-employment tax: SE base = net profit × 92.35%. Social Security = 12.4% of the base up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500. Medicare = 2.9% of the base. Additional Medicare = 0.9% of the base above $200,000.

S corp payroll tax on salary: Social Security = 12.4% of salary up to $184,500, Medicare = 2.9% of salary, Additional Medicare = 0.9% of salary above $200,000. Employer and employee halves are added together because you pay both.

Distributions = profit − salary. They are shown but not subjected to payroll tax.

Payroll and SE tax saved = sole-prop SE tax − S corp FICA. This is the headline.

Assumptions

  • Tax year 2026 Social Security wage base of $184,500 and the $200,000 single-filer Additional Medicare threshold. Married thresholds differ.
  • Salary cannot exceed profit, and the salary you enter is reasonable compensation for the work you actually do.
  • The whole profit is subject to SE tax as a sole proprietor; no wages from a separate W-2 job are using up the wage base.
  • Federal payroll taxes only.

Limitations

  • Income tax is excluded. The deduction for half of SE tax, the QBI deduction (which salary reduces), and the health-insurance deduction interaction can all shrink a thin savings number.
  • State unemployment insurance, payroll service fees, a separate 1120-S return, and state entity taxes are real costs the tool does not subtract.
  • Setting salary too low invites reclassification of distributions as wages. The tool does not judge what is reasonable.
  • Not tax advice.

Frequently asked questions

How much does an S corp save on self-employment tax?+

Roughly the payroll tax on the portion of profit you take as distributions instead of salary, after accounting for the Social Security wage base. The tool shows that number for your profit and salary. Subtract payroll, filing, and state costs before deciding.

What is a reasonable salary for an S corp owner?+

What you would have to pay someone else to do the work you do, considering hours, skills, and local market rates. Document how you arrived at it. Paying yourself a token salary is the most common way S corp savings unravel.

Should a rental property owner elect S corp status?+

Usually not for holding rentals. Rental income is generally not subject to self-employment tax in the first place, so there is nothing to save, and moving appreciated property out of a corporation later is a taxable event. S corps fit operating businesses, including an active short-term rental management or arbitrage business.

Does this include the QBI deduction?+

No. Salary lowers qualified business income but also creates W-2 wages that can support the deduction above the threshold. Run the QBI estimate with both structures to see the net.

Is there a profit level where an S corp starts to make sense?+

There is no fixed number in the law. The election starts to pay when the payroll tax saved on distributions clearly exceeds the added payroll, compliance, and state costs. Put your own cost estimates against this tool's headline.
