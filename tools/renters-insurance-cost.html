What this calculates

Shows the monthly cost and the cost per $1,000 of personal property coverage. Renters insurance is usually cheap relative to what it covers, which is why the per-$1,000 figure is the useful one.

How to use it

  1. Start with annual premium and work down the form, or load an example to begin from a realistic case.
  2. Read the headline result alongside the supporting rows, which show the intermediate figures behind it.
  3. Check the assumptions. They decide what the number includes and, more importantly, what it leaves out.
  4. Sweep personal property coverage on the response curve to see how much it actually moves the answer.
  5. Run a cautious case as well as an optimistic one before using the estimate in a decision.

Common mistakes

  • Skipping renters insurance because it feels like there is nothing worth covering, before doing an inventory.
  • Ignoring the liability portion, which covers far larger risks than the contents ever will.
  • Assuming flood or earthquake damage is included. It generally is not.

Formula

Monthly = annual ÷ 12; cost per $1,000 = annual ÷ (coverage ÷ 1,000)

FAQ

Does my policy cover a roommate's belongings?+

Usually not unless they are named on the policy. Most insurers expect each person to hold their own, and many will not add an unrelated roommate at all. Read the contract before assuming.

Does it cover my things outside the home?+

Typically yes, at a reduced limit. A laptop stolen from a car or a bag taken while travelling is commonly covered, which surprises people who think of it as a home-only policy.

Why does more coverage cost so little extra?+

Because the fixed costs of writing the policy dominate at these premiums, and total losses are rare. Doubling the limit rarely doubles the price, which is exactly what the per-$1,000 figure reveals.

How much does renters insurance cost per month?+

Most renters policies run about $15 to $30 per month. The price depends on your coverage limit, deductible, location, and claims history, and this calculator converts the annual premium to a monthly figure.

Can my landlord require renters insurance?+

Yes. Many leases require it, and some name the landlord on the policy. Premiums are low enough that it rarely becomes a sticking point.

Does renters insurance cover water damage?+

Sudden water damage from a burst pipe is typically covered, but gradual leaks, flooding, and sewer backups usually are not. Flood coverage is a separate policy.

Are these numbers financial advice?+

No. They are educational estimates based on the inputs and assumptions on this page. Confirm important decisions with a qualified professional and your own documents.
