Assumptions

  • Annual pay = amount × the number of times it is paid, then every other figure is derived from that annual total.
  • Biweekly and semimonthly are genuinely different: 26 pay periods against 24, so the same annual salary gives different cheque sizes.
  • Hourly assumes 40 hours a week for 52 weeks.
  • Gross figures. Tax and deductions are not applied.
  • Estimate only. Tax and payroll rules vary by country, state, and year, and this page uses the rates you enter rather than a filing engine's tables.

What this calculates

Annualizes a paycheck based on how often it arrives, then shows every other period equivalent. Useful when a job listing quotes one basis and your budget uses another.

How to use it

  1. Start with pay amount and work down the form, or load an example to begin from a realistic case.
  2. Read the headline result alongside the supporting rows, which show the intermediate figures behind it.
  3. Check the assumptions. They decide what the number includes and, more importantly, what it leaves out.
  4. Sweep pay amount on the response curve to see how much it actually moves the answer.
  5. Run a cautious case as well as an optimistic one before using the estimate in a decision.

Common mistakes

  • Reading a semimonthly stub as though it were biweekly, which overstates annual pay by about 8%.
  • Budgeting monthly on a biweekly income without planning for the two three-paycheck months.
  • Comparing an annual salary to a weekly contract rate without annualizing first.

Formula

Annual = amount × periods a year; every other figure is derived from that annual total

FAQ

Why do biweekly and semimonthly differ?+

Biweekly pays every 14 days, which is 26 cheques a year, so two months contain three paydays. Semimonthly pays on fixed dates twice a month, which is 24 slightly larger cheques.

Is this gross or net pay?+

Whatever you enter. Enter gross and every figure is gross. Use the gross to net pay estimate first if you want take-home equivalents.

Why does the hourly figure assume 40 hours?+

It uses 40 hours across 52 weeks, which is the standard 2,080-hour year. If your real hours differ, the salary to hourly pay calculator lets you set both hours and paid weeks.

Are these numbers financial advice?+

No. They are educational estimates based on the inputs and assumptions on this page. Confirm important decisions with a qualified professional and your own documents.
