What this calculates

Compare monthly net from a short-term rental versus a midterm furnished rental using occupancy, fees, vacancy, and operating costs.

How to use it

  1. Use winter STR occupancy, not July.
  2. Put cleaning and software in STR ops.
  3. If STR wins by $200, that is not a mandate. It is a fragile edge.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing STR gross to midterm net.
  • Leaving utilities out of STR ops.
  • Ignoring 30-day minimum laws.

Formula

STR net = ADR × occupancy × days × (1 − fees) − STR ops. Midterm net = rent × (1 − vacancy) − midterm ops.

FAQ

Why exclude the lease?+

It is the same property either way. The difference is revenue quality and ops.

What occupancy voids an STR win?+

Drop occupancy in this form until the edge flips. That is your risk number.

Are midterms easier to run?+

Usually fewer turns, more credit risk, different insurance. This page is only the cash comparison.

How the midterm vs STR calculator works

Same property, two operating models: nightly short-term rental against a furnished 30-day-plus midterm lease, compared on monthly net.

Method

STR gross after platform fees = nightly rate × occupancy % × (365 ÷ 12) × (1 − platform fee %).

STR monthly net = STR gross after fees − STR monthly operating costs.

Midterm monthly net = midterm rent × (1 − vacancy and turnover void %) − midterm operating costs.

Monthly edge for STR = STR net − midterm net. This is the headline; negative means the midterm lease nets more.

Assumptions

  • Rent or PITI is excluded from both sides because the housing cost is the same property either way. Include it in both operating-cost fields if you prefer whole numbers.
  • STR operating costs include utilities, supplies, software, cleaning you pay, and management; midterm costs include utilities if you cover them, turnover, and management.
  • Pre-tax.

Limitations

  • Regulatory risk is not priced: cities that restrict short stays often leave 30-day-plus rentals untouched, which is a reason to accept a smaller midterm edge.
  • It does not compare tenant credit risk, insurance cost, wear, or your time.
  • Tax treatment differs. A short-term rental with an average stay of seven days or less can be a non-passive activity for a material participant; a midterm rental with 30-day stays is a rental activity subject to the passive loss rules unless you qualify as a real estate professional. That is not in this comparison.
  • Not investment advice.

Frequently asked questions

Is a midterm rental more profitable than Airbnb?+

Sometimes. STR usually wins on gross and loses on costs and turnover. Enter your market's real numbers here and lower STR occupancy until the edge flips; that occupancy is the risk you are taking for the extra income.

Does switching to 30-day stays lose the short term rental loophole?+

Yes. The loophole depends on an average guest stay of seven days or less. Stays of 30 days and longer make it a rental activity, so depreciation losses become passive unless you meet real estate professional status. Weigh that against the operating comparison.

What vacancy should I assume for a midterm rental?+

A void between tenants is normal because 30-day-plus stays turn over less often but leave gaps. The input covers vacancy and turnover void together; a few weeks a year is a reasonable planning starting point, adjusted to your market.

Can I run both models on the same property?+

Many operators do: short stays in peak season and midterm tenants in the off-season. Model each season separately here and blend the results, and keep the average-stay math in mind for tax purposes.
