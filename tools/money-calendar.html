Money Calendar

A simple monthly routine for your investing, taxes, benefits and spending.

Import into Apple, Google or Outlook Calendar. Adds 12 all-day reviews on your chosen day, including past months. These are planning reminders, not official deadlines. Set alerts in your calendar app.

2026 federal dates to check

  • April 15, 2026: usual deadline for 2025 individual federal returns, tax payments and eligible 2025 IRA/HSA contributions.
  • 2026 estimated income tax installments: April 15, June 15, September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027, if required.
  • December 31: review year-end actions in advance. Payroll and provider cutoffs may be earlier; not every retirement contribution has this deadline.

These are general federal dates, not a determination that you owe an installment. Exceptions and disaster relief can change them. Sources: IRS filing dates and IRS estimated tax guidance.

January

Reset your automatic investing

  • Review take-home pay, essential spending and your savings goal.
  • Set automatic transfers that fit your cash flow; check the new year's contribution limits.

February

Gather your tax documents

  • Collect income forms, deductible expense records and HSA receipts.
  • Compare actual spending with your January plan.

March

Check last year's contribution room

  • Review prior-year IRA and HSA eligibility and unused contribution room before the usual April tax-filing deadline.
  • If contributing for last year, select that tax year with your provider. A filing extension generally does not extend this contribution deadline.

April

Use your return to plan ahead

  • Once your return is filed, review what caused a refund or balance due.
  • Adjust current-year withholding or estimated payments with your tax professional if needed.

May

Review your cash cushion

  • Compare emergency savings with essential monthly expenses.
  • Plan ahead for summer travel and other irregular bills.

June

Check your midyear progress

  • Compare contributions and account balances with your annual goals.
  • Adjust automatic transfers for the remaining months if your income or expenses changed.

July

Review debt and recurring costs

  • Review loan rates, balances and your next payoff priority.
  • Cancel subscriptions you no longer use and compare recurring bills.

August

Review who and what is protected

  • Check beneficiaries and important account information.
  • Review insurance coverage after major life changes.

September

Forecast income and taxes

  • Estimate full-year income, including bonuses and side income.
  • Check withholding and estimated payments before year-end with your tax professional.

October

Set your holiday budget

  • Choose spending amounts for gifts, travel and celebrations.
  • Move money into a dedicated spending category before shopping.

November

Review employer benefits

  • Compare next year's health plan, HSA or FSA options and retirement benefits.
  • Confirm your employer's actual enrollment dates; they vary by plan.

December

Finish your year-end review

  • Check remaining workplace retirement contributions and payroll cutoffs; review planned giving.
  • Review realized gains and losses with a tax professional before trading. Check wash-sale rules and each action's deadline.

Use your own deadlines

This is a personal planning calendar. Confirm federal and state tax dates, disaster extensions, employer enrollment windows and provider cutoffs separately. Prior-year contributions depend on eligibility; IRA contribution eligibility and deductibility are different questions. Rules were reviewed in September 2026; recheck them for 2027.

Sources: IRS IRA reminders, IRS HSA contribution rules.

How this money calendar works

The calendar turns a year of money admin into twelve short monthly reviews with saved progress and optional calendar reminders.

Method

Each month has one review task with two prompts, ordered around the U.S. tax and benefits year: reset automatic investing in January, gather tax documents in February, check prior-year IRA and HSA contribution room in March, use the filed return to adjust withholding in April, then work through your cash cushion, midyear progress, debt and recurring costs, beneficiaries and insurance, income and tax forecasting, holiday budgeting, employer benefits, and a year-end review.

You choose a planning year (2026 or 2027) and a monthly review day from 1 to 28. The .ics download adds twelve all-day reminders on that day to Apple, Google, or Outlook Calendar, including past months so the file is complete.

Marking a month Done updates the completed count for that year. Progress is stored in your browser on this device and can be reset per year.

Assumptions

  • You are a U.S. taxpayer following the federal calendar. The dates listed (the usual April 15 filing and payment deadline, the quarterly estimated-payment dates, and December 31 year-end cutoffs) are general federal dates from IRS guidance as of September 2026.
  • Review days stop at 28 so the same day exists in every month.
  • The monthly prompts are a general routine, not a personalized plan, and assume a typical household with employer benefits, retirement accounts, and some taxable income.

Limitations

  • The reminders are planning prompts, not official deadlines. State tax dates, disaster-relief extensions, employer enrollment windows, and provider cutoffs are not included and must be confirmed separately.
  • It does not calculate whether you owe estimated tax installments, whether you are eligible to contribute to an IRA or HSA, or whether a contribution is deductible.
  • Progress lives in browser storage, so it does not sync across devices or browsers, and clearing site data removes it.
  • Rules were reviewed for 2026. Contribution limits, deadlines, and enrollment dates can change, so recheck them before relying on the 2027 calendar.

Sources

  • IRS: IRA year-end reminders
  • IRS Publication 969: Health Savings Accounts

Frequently asked questions

Does the money calendar send me reminders?+

Not by itself. Download the .ics file and import it into your calendar app, then set alerts there. The file contains twelve all-day events on your chosen review day.

Will my progress carry over to another device?+

No. Completed months are saved in this browser on this device only. If you switch devices or clear browsing data, the checklist starts empty for that year.

Are the dates on this page official tax deadlines?+

They are general federal dates, such as the usual April 15 individual filing deadline and the quarterly estimated-payment dates, taken from IRS guidance when the page was reviewed in September 2026. They are not a determination that you owe an installment, and exceptions or disaster relief can change them, so confirm with the IRS or your tax professional.

Which months matter most for taxes?+

February for gathering income forms and receipts, March for checking prior-year IRA and HSA room before the April deadline, April for using the filed return to adjust withholding or estimates, September for forecasting full-year income and payments, and December for year-end contributions, payroll cutoffs, and realized gains and losses.
