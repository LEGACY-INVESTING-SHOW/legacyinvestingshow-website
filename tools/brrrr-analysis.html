What this calculates

Estimate cash left in after an ARV refinance, then monthly cash flow and cash-on-cash on the remaining cash.

How to use it

  1. If cash left in is negative, the deal cash-out refinances. Confirm the lender allows it.
  2. ARV is the number that makes or breaks BRRRR. Be boring.
  3. Rerun at 70% LTV if 75% feels like a brochure.

Common mistakes

  • Using retail ARV on a house that will still look like a rehab.
  • Forgetting holding costs during rehab.
  • Underwriting rent from Zillow without comps.

Formula

Cash left in = purchase + rehab − ARV × refi LTV. Cash flow uses the new amortizing payment.

FAQ

What if cash left in is negative?+

You pulled money out. CoC is not a useful percentage then; look at cash flow and risk.

Is 75% LTV realistic?+

Sometimes. Many investors underwrite 70% so the refinance still closes.

Does this include the original hard-money loan?+

No. It assumes you get to the long-term refinance. Bridge costs belong in rehab/holding.

How the BRRRR analysis calculator works

Buy, rehab, rent, refinance, repeat: how much of your cash a refinance at the after-repair value returns, and what cash flow the new loan leaves.

Method

All-in cost = purchase price + rehab and holding costs.

Refinance proceeds = after-repair value × refinance LTV.

Cash left in = all-in cost − refinance proceeds. This is the headline; a negative number means the refinance returned more than you put in.

The new monthly payment is computed on the refinance loan at the rate and term you enter.

Monthly cash flow after refinance = rent × (1 − vacancy %) − monthly operating costs − new payment.

Cash-on-cash on remaining cash = annual cash flow ÷ cash left in, shown only when cash left in is positive.

Assumptions

  • The refinance appraises at your ARV and the lender lends the LTV you enter. Appraisal risk is not simulated.
  • Purchase closing costs, bridge or hard-money interest, and refinance closing costs are inside rehab and holding if you put them there.
  • Rent and operating costs are stabilized post-rehab figures.
  • Pre-tax; no depreciation or income tax.

Limitations

  • Seasoning requirements, cash-out caps, and lender limits on how many financed properties you can hold are not modeled and often decide whether the refinance happens at all.
  • A negative cash left in does not mean free money: the loan is bigger and the cushion thinner. Judge the deal on cash flow and DSCR after the refinance.
  • Rehab overruns and holding-period delays are the usual failure mode and are only captured if you pad the rehab input.
  • Not investment advice.

Frequently asked questions

What LTV should I assume for a BRRRR refinance?+

Many investors underwrite 70% to 75% of ARV for a cash-out refinance on a single-family rental, with the exact number depending on the lender, property type, and your credit. Run the tool at the lower figure too; if the deal only works at the higher LTV, it is fragile.

How much cash should be left in a BRRRR deal?+

Getting all of it back is the goal, not the rule. A deal that leaves some cash in but cash-flows well after the new loan can beat one that returns every dollar and runs at break-even.

Does BRRRR create tax savings?+

The rehab that becomes part of the building depreciates over 27.5 years, while components a cost segregation study classifies as 5-, 7-, or 15-year property can qualify for bonus depreciation. Refinance proceeds themselves are not taxable income. Whether depreciation losses offset your W-2 income depends on the passive loss rules.

How do I estimate ARV honestly?+

Use closed sales of renovated comparable homes from the last few months in the same submarket, and discount if your finish level is lower. A retail ARV on a rental-grade rehab is the most common way a BRRRR gets stuck with cash trapped in the deal.
