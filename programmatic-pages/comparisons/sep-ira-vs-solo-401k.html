SEP IRA vs Solo 401(k)

Choose the best retirement plan for your self-employed business

Feature SEP IRA Solo 401(k)
2026 Contribution Limit Up to $69,000 (25% of compensation) Up to $69,000 ($23,500 elective + employer match)
Catch-Up (50+) No catch-up contribution Additional $7,500 ($77,000 total)
Setup Cost $0 - Free at most brokerages $0-$500 depending on provider
Administrative Complexity Low - Minimal paperwork Medium - Annual Form 5500-EZ required
Loan Option No loans permitted Yes - Borrow up to $50,000
Roth Option No Roth SEP IRA Yes - Roth Solo 401(k) available
Best For High earners wanting simplicity Maximizing contributions, Roth benefits

When to Choose SEP IRA

  • You want the simplest retirement plan with minimal paperwork
  • You have variable income and want flexibility in annual contributions
  • You don't need catch-up contributions (under 50)
  • You have employees and need to contribute equally for all
  • You want to minimize administrative costs and complexity

When to Choose Solo 401(k)

  • You want to maximize retirement contributions (especially under $300K income)
  • You're 50+ and want catch-up contributions
  • You want Roth contribution options for tax-free growth
  • You might need a loan from your retirement account
  • You have no employees (other than spouse)

The Bottom Line

Solo 401(k) wins for most self-employed individuals because it allows higher contributions at lower income levels and offers Roth options. However, if you have employees or want the absolute simplest solution, a SEP IRA is still an excellent choice.

Quick Decision Framework

  • Income under $200K? → Solo 401(k) maximizes your savings
  • Over 50 years old? → Solo 401(k) for catch-up contributions
  • Want Roth option? → Solo 401(k) only
  • Have employees? → SEP IRA (must include them equally)
  • Want minimal paperwork? → SEP IRA
