Traditional vs Roth 401(k)

Understanding the tax implications to maximize your retirement savings

Feature Traditional 401(k) Roth 401(k)
Tax Treatment (Now) Tax-deductible contributions reduce current taxable income Contributions made with after-tax dollars
Tax Treatment (Later) Withdrawals taxed as ordinary income in retirement Tax-free withdrawals in retirement (after age 59½)
2026 Contribution Limit $23,500 ($30,500 if 50+) $23,500 ($30,500 if 50+)
Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs) Required starting at age 73 Required starting at age 73 (can roll to Roth IRA to avoid)
Early Withdrawal Taxed + 10% penalty before 59½ Contributions can be withdrawn tax-free anytime; earnings taxed + 10% penalty before 59½
Employer Match Always pre-tax (traditional) Always pre-tax (traditional)
Best For Higher earners in peak earning years Younger workers, lower current tax bracket

The Traditional 401(k) Advantage

Immediate Tax Savings

If you're in the 32% tax bracket and contribute $23,500, you save $7,520 in taxes this year. This gives you more money to invest or pay off high-interest debt.

Lower Current Tax Bracket

Reducing your taxable income might drop you into a lower bracket, providing even more tax savings on your entire income, not just your contribution.

Flexibility in Retirement

If you retire in a lower tax bracket, you'll pay less tax on withdrawals than you saved on contributions—a double win.

The Roth 401(k) Advantage

Tax-Free Growth

All growth—whether from contributions, employer match (which is always traditional), or investment returns—is completely tax-free in retirement. A $1M Roth balance is actually worth $1M.

No Tax Surprises

You know exactly what you'll have in retirement. With Traditional, your future tax rate is unknown—a risk if tax rates rise.

No RMD Worries (with strategy)

While Roth 401(k)s have RMDs, you can roll to a Roth IRA at retirement to avoid them completely—keeping money growing tax-free indefinitely.

Real Numbers: Which Saves More?

Scenario: $100K Income, 24% Tax Bracket

Traditional 401(k): Contribute $23,500, save $5,640 in taxes this year. At retirement in 15% bracket, withdrawal tax = $3,525. Net benefit: $2,115

Scenario: Same Growth, Different Tax

Roth 401(k): Contribute $23,500 after-tax (costs $30,921 pre-tax). No taxes ever again. At 32% retirement tax rate, Roth wins by $3,760.

The Verdict: How to Decide

Choose Traditional If:

  • You're in a high tax bracket now (24%+)
  • You expect to be in a lower bracket in retirement
  • You need the tax break to afford contributions
  • You want to reduce current adjusted gross income (AGI)

Choose Roth If:

  • You're early in your career (lower tax bracket now)
  • You expect tax rates to rise in the future
  • You want tax diversification in retirement
  • You have a pension or other taxable retirement income
