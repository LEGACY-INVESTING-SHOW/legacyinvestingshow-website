What works

Nothing competes with the three numbers. The whole answer fits on one phone screen with the disclaimer, which is the brief's mobile requirement.

What risks

The rental row reads as "rent is tax free" unless the person taps "Show the math". The paper-loss explanation lives below the fold.

Pick this if

You want the DM link to land on the carousel's punchline in under three seconds and trust people to dig for the why.

What works

Every number is on the page and the rental steps read top to bottom the way the brief lists them. Nothing to decorate, so nothing to look generic.

What risks

It is a long scroll on a phone and the three totals do not jump out. Someone skimming from Instagram may not find the comparison.

Pick this if

The audience is the skeptic who wants to check the math, and you would rather be trusted than be flashy.

What works

Each screen has one number and one story. The rental steps get the room they need without crowding the other two paths.

What risks

The comparison is the whole point of the carousel, and here it needs three swipes. The teaser line under the dots has to carry it.

Pick this if

You expect people to arrive already sold on the idea and want to inspect one path, usually the rental one, in detail.

How to pick a direction

Pick on tradeoff, not vibes. All four run the same math, split mode, and editable assumptions behind a link.

Fastest read →A. Three lines. The answer fits one screen.
Most credible →B. Statement. Every line item, no decoration.
Most shareable →C. One chart. The picture is the argument.
Deepest per path →D. One path at a time. Big number, then its steps.
