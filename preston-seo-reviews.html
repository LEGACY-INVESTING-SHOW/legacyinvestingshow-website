Who the Legacy Wealth Blueprint is for, and who it is not for

It is built for you if

  • You earn roughly $100K to $200K or more from a salary, self-employment, or a business, and you suspect your return is leaving strategies on the table.
  • You want one written plan that covers taxes, debt, income, investments, and protection, in the order to do them, rather than tactics collected from videos.
  • You keep your CPA and want a brief to hand them before filing season, not a new preparer.
  • You will show up to weekly calls and bi-weekly check-ins and do the implementation between them.
  • You are a business owner deciding how to structure and pay yourself, or a high earner paying more tax than you expected to.

It is not for you if

  • You want tax preparation or filing. The program does not file returns and does not contact your CPA.
  • You want passive, index-fund-only advice with no changes to entities, accounts, debt, or income.
  • You will not implement. The plan is written for you; the actions are yours.
  • You need a published price before any conversation. Pricing is only discussed on the strategy call.
  • You want a done-for-you property purchase or a side business first. Those are different programs (STR Concierge and Airbnb Ascension); ask on the call which one fits.
  • You expect the case-study numbers. They are individual and self-reported, and one of the four named clients reports no figure at all.

What the Legacy Wealth Blueprint includes

Lifetime access to the video curriculum. Twelve months of live coaching, community, and a dedicated guide. The list below is taken from the program page.

  • A custom 12-month Wealth Strategy Snapshot: a written financial action plan built from your income, accounts, debt, and goals, which says what to do first.
  • 100+ video lessons across five pillars, with lifetime access: tax optimisation, debt and credit leverage, income diversification, investment vehicles, and protection.
  • Weekly live coaching calls with replays. Every past recording is included.
  • A dedicated Legacy Wealth Guide: an onboarding call, one direct point of contact, and an accountability check-in every two weeks.
  • The Wealth Circle private community for twelve months.
  • An optional Prior Year Tax Return Diagnostic: a short review of previous returns that identifies missed strategies, delivered in weeks two to three.
  • A CPA Implementation Brief, delivered around months ten to eleven, that you hand to your own tax professional before filing season. The team does not contact your CPA directly.
  • The Legacy Wealth AI Advisor, plus SOPs, templates, and checklists: a CPA vetting checklist, a tax planning timeline, a debt settlement guide, and a financial freedom calculator.
  • A partner network at client pricing (tax strategist, estate attorney, credit advisor, entity setup partner), including a 45 to 60 minute private strategy session. Confirm scope and any separate fees before you engage one.
  • A second membership for a spouse or direct family member at no extra charge.

The five curriculum pillars

  1. Tax optimisation: entity structures (LLC, S-Corp), deductions and write-offs, common mistakes, tax-advantaged accounts, W-2 and business tax strategies.
  2. Debt and credit leverage: debt elimination systems, negotiating, refinancing and balance transfers, credit repair, business credit.
  3. Income diversification: side business frameworks, passive income, income stacking, funding strategies.
  4. Investment vehicles: stock market basics, real estate step by step, retirement accounts (401(k), Roth, HSA, and lesser-known accounts).
  5. Protection: asset protection, insurance optimisation, estate planning and trusts, hiring the right advisors, portfolio automation.

How to read reviews of a coaching program

  1. Separate the free events from the paid program. Many Trustpilot reviews describe the three-day tax challenge; the Legacy Wealth Blueprint is a twelve-month membership. Read each review for which one it covers.

  2. Weight independent sources over first-party ones. Trustpilot and the BBB are outside Preston's control. The video interviews are his recordings, so treat them as detailed, named, and first-party.

  3. Look for actions, not just outcomes. Stephanie Dailey's case study lists what she did: business bank accounts, real books, a startup cost deduction, kids on payroll. A review that names actions is easier to test against your own situation.

  4. Read the worst reviews last, and in full. The four complaints they raise (refunds, replies, a moving price, first-party testimonials) are the questions to bring to the call.

  5. Apply the disclaimer literally. Every published result is self-reported and individual. If a figure would only be worth it to you at the top of the range, assume you will not get the top of the range.
