What the programs include

Program deliverables named in the case studies match what the programs publish. The Legacy Wealth Blueprint pairs a video curriculum with a custom twelve-month Wealth Strategy Snapshot (the personalized action plan clients call their implementation plan), weekly live coaching, a 1-on-1 Legacy Wealth Guide, an optional Prior Year Tax Return Diagnostic, a CPA Implementation Brief the client hands to their own CPA before filing season, and a partner network for entity, estate and tax work. Airbnb Ascension is built on the Airbnb Arbitrage Roadmap curriculum and runs a three-step process: acquire the lease, set up operations, automate pricing and systems, with a dedicated STR coach, landlord scripts, deal-review reports and community support. Pricing is not published; it is discussed on the strategy call.

Stephanie Dailey: over $20,000 saved in taxes in her first year

Legacy Wealth Blueprint · Video interview on /reviews · Full case study

Before

  • Software engineers, both more than 20 years in the field; she works in identity and security architecture and consults on the side
  • Household over $300,000 in W-2 income plus contract work; five kids in a mix of private school and homeschooling
  • Invested, saved for college, carried the right insurance, drove Toyotas, and still had nothing left: "There are no slush funds"
  • Her old CPA was not a fan of putting kids on payroll
  • Cautious buyer: two to four months between first contact and joining

After

  • Over $20,000 saved in taxes soon after joining (Preston's opening line in the interview); her written note calls it a $20k tax refund after implementing the strategies
  • $27,000 startup cost deduction captured; over $60,000 of bridge-loan mortgage interest deducted
  • Expects $40,000 to $50,000 over the next twelve months, conservatively (a forecast she gave when asked for a ballpark)
  • House payoff horizon moved from about ten years to seven or eight
  • Her husband quit his job to go all in on their startups, time-boxed to six months

How

  • Reviewed the accounts once a week, which surfaced subscriptions to cancel and expenses landing on the wrong card
  • Started keeping real books for the 2025 tax year and opened separate business bank accounts
  • Captured startup costs she says she would not have known were deductible
  • Sent the bridge-loan interest to her preparer instead of only the standard mortgage form
  • Put her kids on payroll through Gusto, running it small in 2025 to test it before ramping up in 2026
  • Used the program's entity-setup partner

“we were just really blown away by how quickly it paid off”

Albert: net worth growth of 25% and home insurance cut in half

Legacy Wealth Blueprint · Video interview on /reviews · Full case study

Before

  • ER physician working mainly in Orange County with shifts in the Central Valley and Northern California; finished residency in 2021
  • About $5,000 in a Roth and nothing else for retirement before 2021
  • Followed the standard advice afterwards: max the 401(k), use the physician group's defined benefit plan (maybe 6% a year at most), fund the HSA
  • Had never heard of the backdoor Roth IRA, tax loss harvesting or the HSA receipt strategy
  • Combined California tax bracket of almost 50%, federal and state

After

  • Net worth up 25%, with about $450,000 named in the same breath, reported exactly as spoken
  • Self-managed 401(k) up about 19% year to date; Roth up about 40%
  • Home insurance cut in half about a month before the interview
  • EIN, entity and business checking account in place for a peptide wellness telemedicine clinic
  • He notes himself that starting to earn in 2021 means part of the growth is time in the market on a physician income

How

  • Treated the program like med school: notes on everything, plus his own action-task list at the end of each video
  • Used crypto losses to offset capital gains and some ordinary income through tax loss harvesting
  • Pays medical costs out of pocket, files the receipts in a Google folder and leaves the HSA untouched
  • Opened a backdoor Roth IRA and moved his portfolio off professional management, backing trades with data and keeping a cash hedge
  • Shopped his home insurance and moved cash from a 0.1% bank savings account to high-yield savings
  • Followed the program's framework for a new business: EIN, entity, the right structure for the state

Airbnb Arbitrage · Video interview on /reviews · Full case study

Before

  • Data-driven by training, with a long-standing interest in construction and design

  • Learns from other operators rather than working it out alone

How to read these numbers

Every figure is self-reported

Each number is what the client said in a recorded interview or wrote in their own note. The written case studies print the timestamp beside each figure so you can hear it in context. Nothing on this page has been independently audited, and no figure was added or rounded up.

A calculated ROI is not a realized tax saving

Stephanie's over $20,000 is a reported saving after implementing the strategies; her written note calls it a $20k tax refund. Abigail's just under $100,000 is her own forecast for the year, run twice, not a banked number. Albert's 25% is a net worth change he names, not a tax figure. Shawn gives no figure at all. Quote each one with the label the client gave it.

Bookings are not profit

Tom's $25,000 in two weeks, Lindsay's $17,500 in 14 days and Hampton's $20,000 in a month are revenue booked, not cash cleared. Marchia and Cash's $6,000 April is gross against $3,200 rent. Figures described as cash flow or profit (Rob's $10,000, Gary's $35,000, Dustin's $6,000, Kiana's $3,200) are after expenses as the client stated them.

Forecasts are flagged as forecasts

Stephanie's $40,000 to $50,000 for the following year, Michael's $12,000 a month, Andrew's $2,500 a month in year two and Micah's $8,000 a month goal are projections the client gave. They are listed because the client said them, not because they happened.

Local short-term rental rules, landlord call volume, available capital, seasonality and how fast the client acts explain most of the spread.
