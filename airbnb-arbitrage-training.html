What rental arbitrage is

You sign a long-term lease, usually 12 months, on a property in a market where short stays are legal, get the landlord's written permission to sublet it nightly, furnish it, list it on Airbnb, VRBO and Booking.com, and keep the difference between nightly revenue and your rent, utilities, cleaning, supplies and platform fees. The target most operators use is cash flow of roughly two to three times the monthly lease cost.

Your money at risk is the deposit, the furniture and the rent you owe whether or not you get bookings. You build no equity and get no depreciation on a building you do not own. In exchange you start with far less capital than buying, and you can add units without a down payment. Two gates decide whether a deal works, and no course controls either: whether short stays are legal in that zone, and whether the landlord will put permission in the lease.

That is why the ten-point checklist below starts with regulation and consent, and why the failure modes are mostly about underwriting and operations rather than marketing.

What a legitimate arbitrage training must cover

Seven modules. A course missing any of them is incomplete; a coaching program missing any of them is overpriced.

  1. The landlord pitch

    How to find owners and property managers, what to say, how to handle no, and how to present yourself as the tenant who will maintain the place better than most. Practised by roleplay. One documented client rehearsed the script with his father for an hour and a half before his calls; another made 50 to 70 calls before signing. See how to convince landlords.

  2. Lease clauses

    Written permission to sublet for short stays, term and renewal, what happens on assignment, deposit terms, and who handles what. A sublease addendum template and a lease checklist are the minimum a course should hand you.

  3. Market selection and rules

    How to check zoning, licensing and minimum-stay rules for a specific address, how to read demand data, and how to pick a market where the rent-to-nightly-rate gap works. See how to evaluate a city and whether arbitrage is legal where you are.

  4. The furnishing budget

    A line-item budget, a supplier list, what guests actually notice, and how to set up remotely. See the furniture budget checklist and the startup cost breakdown.

  5. Pricing

    A floor price from your fixed and variable costs, launch pricing to earn the first reviews, and dynamic pricing tools. Underwrite at 50% occupancy before you sign anything.

  6. Guest operations

    Screening, check-in, messaging templates, a cleaning team with a backup, supplies and reviews. See guest message templates.

  7. Taxes and the entity

    Arbitrage income is business income: separate accounts, bookkeeping, the deductions that apply (furniture, supplies, platform fees, a home office where it qualifies), lodging tax collection, and when an entity makes sense. A course does not replace a CPA; it should tell you when to hire one.

A ten-point checklist for vetting any arbitrage course

Score a program out of ten. Below seven, keep looking.

  1. It makes you check local rules first.

    Zoning, licensing, minimum stays, primary-residence requirements, lodging tax. Before the pitch, not after.

  2. It puts landlord consent in the lease.

    A sublease addendum or clause, not a verbal yes. It hands you the template.

  3. It underwrites at 50% occupancy.

    And shows you the model. If the numbers only work at 80%, it says so.

  4. It teaches the pitch and makes you practise it.

    Scripts plus roleplay, and an honest account of how many calls a first lease takes.

  5. It gives you a furnishing budget with line items.

    Not 'about a few thousand'. A tracker you fill in.

  6. It covers pricing beyond 'use a tool'.

    A floor price from your costs, launch pricing, seasonality, and when to raise.

  7. It covers guest operations and the cleaning team.

    Screening, templates, a primary and a backup cleaner, a supplies list.

  8. It names real students you can watch or speak to.

    With dates and markets. No names, no proof.

  9. It tells you when to hire a CPA and what to ask.

    Separate accounts, bookkeeping, lodging tax, entity timing.

  10. It puts total, term and refund terms in writing when you ask.

    Before the call. Sellers who will not are telling you how they sell.

Common failure modes, and what good training teaches instead

Adapted from our guide to the ten arbitrage mistakes that kill profit. Most new operators fail on these, not on the model.

Failure modeWhat a good program teaches instead
Signing the lease before running the numbersMap every dollar in and out, including occupancy tax, platform fees and a wear-and-tear reserve, at 50% occupancy. Walk away if it only works at 80%.
Taking the first lease terms offeredNegotiate a 12-month term with a renewal option and written permission to sublet. If the landlord bans subletting, walk; do not build on a clause that can end you.
Ignoring local regulationCheck the city clerk, the planning department and the zoning for the exact address before listing. Non-compliance ends the business, not just the margin.
Underestimating cleaningBudget a realistic per-turnover figure for your market, and keep two cleaning teams, one primary and one backup.
Pricing below break-even to win early bookingsCompute a floor from fixed plus variable costs divided by realistic nights, price competitively at launch, then raise after the first 10 to 15 reviews with a dynamic tool.
No cash reserveThree months of rent, utilities and operating costs before you start. Lumpy cash flow and a dead air-conditioner are normal.
Poor guest screeningUse the platform's tools, read reviews and profiles, and decline the bookings that look wrong. Selective hosts keep their ratings and their furniture.
Neglecting the guest experienceClear check-in, accurate photos, good wifi, fast replies. The first five reviews decide the listing.
Mixing personal and business moneyA separate account, every expense tracked, profit per booking known. Guessing hides losses.
Quitting in month fourPlan for a 12-month ramp. The first months are for reviews, market learning and operations, not profit.

Who should not buy any arbitrage training

Six readers who should keep their money for now.

  • Your target city bans it. Primary-residence rules or 30-day minimums end the model. Move markets or change model.
  • The fee would be your working capital. Deposits, furniture and the reserve come first.
  • You hate the phone and will not practise. The landlord call is the business. One documented client who disliked calls pitched owners face to face instead; if you will do neither, this is not for you.
  • You want passive income from month one. Launch is work. Two to four hours a week comes after systems are built.
  • You want equity or the owner-only tax benefits. Buy instead; see the STR coaching guide.
  • You expect a guarantee. Nobody can promise occupancy or a property count.

Questions people ask

What is the best Airbnb arbitrage training?

For a working professional who wants a first unit live within a few months and someone to check the lease and the numbers before signing, Airbnb Ascension, built on the Airbnb Arbitrage Roadmap, is what we built for that, and it is ours. For someone still deciding, a cheap self-paced course with a published refund policy, such as Sean Rakidzich's, is the sensible first step. Score any option against the ten-point checklist.

What is Airbnb arbitrage, and is it legal?

You lease a property long term, get written landlord permission to sublet it nightly, furnish it and list it. It is legal where short stays are allowed in that zone and the lease permits it. Many cities require registration, a licence number and lodging tax, and some require the host to live there, which rules arbitrage out. Check the exact address before you sign.

How much capital do I need before any training?

Enough for the deposit, the furniture and a few months of rent in reserve, separate from any program fee. Our startup cost breakdown and furniture budget checklist walk through the line items. If the program fee would use up that reserve, buy a course and keep your cash.

Is the Airbnb Arbitrage Roadmap sold separately from Airbnb Ascension?

No. The Roadmap is the 7+ hour self-paced curriculum inside Airbnb Ascension, with lifetime access. Ascension adds the dedicated coach, the Property Profitability Reports, weekly live calls, the community and the done-for-you setup pieces. Pricing is discussed on the strategy call and is not published.

How many landlord calls does it take to sign a first lease?

In the documented client stories on this site: one couple signed on their fourth call, another client after 50 to 70 calls, another lined up five interested landlords in a single weekend of full-day calling. Plan for dozens of conversations and a script you have practised out loud.

What does arbitrage do to my taxes?

It is business income, not passive rental income: separate accounts, bookkeeping, deductions for furniture, supplies and fees, and lodging tax collection where required. It does not give you depreciation on a building you do not own, so the STR loophole for W-2 income belongs to owners. Confirm your treatment with a CPA.

What are the most common ways new arbitrage operators lose money?

Signing before running the numbers at 50% occupancy, accepting lease terms without written sublet permission, ignoring local rules, underestimating cleaning, pricing below break-even, having no reserve, weak guest screening, a poor guest experience, mixed finances, and quitting before month twelve.
