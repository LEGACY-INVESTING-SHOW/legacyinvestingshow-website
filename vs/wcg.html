Quick verdict

This is not a like-for-like comparison, and pretending otherwise would waste your time. WCG CPAs is a Colorado-based CPA firm: you hire it and it does recurring work, tax planning, return preparation, and for small-business owners the S-corp payroll and bookkeeping that keep an entity compliant. Legacy Wealth Blueprint is a 12-month coached education program: you learn the strategies, get a written plan and a 1-on-1 guide, and hand a CPA Implementation Brief to whichever CPA files for you, which could be WCG.

If you already run an S-corp, or are about to, and want someone else to own payroll, reasonable-salary support and the filings, hire a firm like WCG and read its knowledge base; it is genuinely good. If your problem is that you do not understand your own money well enough to direct a CPA, or your issues run wider than one entity, join a program. If you are a six-figure business owner who wants to understand the strategy and have it executed, do both, in that order.

What WCG CPAs is

A Colorado-based CPA firm best known for small-business and S-corporation tax planning. It has an unusually detailed public knowledge base on LLCs, S-corp elections, reasonable salary, accountable plans and multi-state issues, and its team has published a widely read guide on LLCs and S corps. Much of what people know about S-corp mechanics online traces back to material like this.

Format. A CPA firm engagement: typically packaged annual services bundling tax planning, business and personal return preparation, and often payroll and bookkeeping for S-corp owners, plus one-off consultations. Fees vary by scope. Verify the current packages, what is bundled, and which states and entity types it serves before engaging.

Known for

  • S-corp election, reasonable-salary and payroll planning for owners and consultants
  • A deep, free public knowledge base and a well-known book on LLCs and S corps
  • Bundled annual engagements that cover planning and compliance together

Best for

  • Owners of an S-corp, or a business that should become one, who want compliance and planning under one roof
  • Consultants and professionals with pass-through income and multi-state questions
  • People who want a firm with a written point of view, not just a preparer

Not ideal for

  • W-2 earners with no business, who give an S-corp specialist little to do
  • People whose problems are wider than the entity: debt, retirement accounts, insurance, estate, a first side income
  • Anyone who wants to understand and drive the strategy rather than delegate it

Why people compare WCG CPAs and Legacy Wealth Blueprint

  • They found WCG's knowledge base, learned a lot, and are deciding whether to hire a firm or run it themselves with support.
  • They do not yet have a business, so an S-corp specialist has nothing to work with.
  • Their CPA files on time but never brings a strategy, and they want to know what to ask for.
  • Their questions are wider than one entity: debt, retirement accounts, insurance, a rental, a spouse's income.
  • They want a written plan they own and understand, not only a filed return.

Where Legacy Wealth Blueprint does not fit

Legacy Wealth Blueprint does not replace a CPA firm and does not try to. Nobody on the team files returns, runs S-corp payroll, handles state notices or gives legal advice, and the team does not contact your CPA; you hand over the brief. If your need is compliance and execution for an entity you already have, a firm like WCG is the right buy and a program is the wrong one.

It is also the wrong fit if you earn well under six figures, if paying would come out of an emergency fund, or if you only want the S-corp question answered. For that single question, WCG's knowledge base, our S-corp strategy page and our S-corp vs sole proprietorship estimator get you most of the way without paying either of us.

How to decide

  1. Answer one question first: do I need a return filed and an entity kept compliant this year? If yes, you need a firm regardless of anything else.

  2. Answer a second: do I want to understand the strategy or delegate it? Delegate means firm. Understand means program. Both means program first.

  3. List what you own. W-2 only points to a program. An S-corp with payroll points to a firm. Business plus rentals plus household finance points to both.

  4. Size the S-corp decision before either call with our estimator and the S-corp vs QBI comparison. If the election is not worth it yet, a firm has less to do.

  5. Ask a firm like WCG: what is bundled, who does my work, how reasonable salary is set and documented, which states are covered, how often we talk outside filing season, and whether they will execute a strategy brief from outside.

  6. Ask Legacy Wealth Blueprint on the strategy call: what the Wealth Strategy Snapshot contains, who my guide is, how the Prior Year Tax Return Diagnostic and the CPA Implementation Brief work, what the No Client Left Behind Guarantee requires in writing, and what the partner network charges separately.

Questions people ask

Is WCG CPAs good for S-corp owners?

It is one of the better-known firms for that niche, with a public knowledge base on reasonable salary, accountable plans and multi-state issues that many other preparers quote. Whether it is right for you depends on the package, the states covered and how much planning contact you get outside filing season. Ask directly and verify the current offering.

Should I hire a CPA firm or take a tax strategy program?

Hire a firm if you need returns filed and an entity kept compliant and you are happy to delegate. Take a program if you want to understand and direct your plan, especially when your questions go beyond one entity. Do both if you are a six-figure owner who wants the plan and the execution, program first so the firm engagement is productive.

Can Legacy Wealth Blueprint replace my CPA?

No. It is education plus coaching with a written plan. It does not file returns, run payroll or give legal advice. It gives you a CPA Implementation Brief for your own CPA before filing season, and your CPA decides what applies. If you have no CPA, the partner network includes a tax strategist at client pricing; confirm scope and fees before engaging.

What should I ask a CPA firm before hiring them for S-corp work?

What is in the annual package and what is billed separately. Who does my work day to day. How reasonable salary is set and documented. Which states and entity types are covered. How often we talk outside filing season. Whether they will execute a strategy brief from outside the firm. What happens if I want to leave.

When do I need both a firm and a program?

When you have real business income that needs compliance and your questions go beyond the entity: debt, retirement accounts, a rental, a spouse's income, protection. The program builds the plan and the brief; the firm executes the entity and files. Six-figure owners with a mix of assets are the usual case.

How much does WCG CPAs cost, and how much is Legacy Wealth Blueprint?

WCG's fees vary by package and scope; check its site for current tiers. Legacy Wealth Blueprint does not publish a price; it is discussed on the strategy call with what is included. Neither figure should be your first question. Decide firm, program or both, then price the one you need.
